# Lecture 6 - Artificial Intelligence - Part I · notes
- [00:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=40s) Hamming: Notice the personal stakes behind an argument about intelligence: "you don't like to feel that you are inferior to machines which you have built". (context: He names ego and religious convictions as obstacles to judging machine capabilities. His joke about predicting the world's end in 2000 or 2001 asks why God should count in base ten rather than base two; it illustrates projecting human habits onto something else, not a demonstrated account of anyone's beliefs.)
- [03:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=228s) Hamming: Do not move the test after a machine passes it: successful performance becomes "merely a formula", and success itself "causes you to deny that that can be thinking". (context: Chess was accepted as requiring thought before programs could play it. Once the mechanism is visible, people withdraw the credit they had promised.)
- [05:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=324s) Hamming: A proposed explanation must face how it works: "how do they interact"? (context: If ideas or a soul are nonmaterial, he asks how they alter physical molecules; if ideas are material, he asks how the brain stores them. He presents difficulties for both positions, not a proof that either is false.)
- [06:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=385s) Hamming: "I do not care what you think I do care that you do think"; learn to express a coherent position. (context: He plans a student discussion of whether machines can think and imagines an admiral's husband asking a computing graduate the question socially. The exercise is to reason and explain, rather than repeat the professor's answer.)
- [07:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=449s) Hamming: Keep both possibilities open: "both believe and disbelieve at the same time". (context: An organizational leader who assumes a fundamental human-machine divide may miss useful applications; one who assumes machines can think may try too much and fall flat. He wants ambition tempered by doubt, not automatic acceptance or rejection.)
- [08:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=480s) Hamming: Choose a testing ground where success is decidable: in games "the object is clear" and "the rules are clear". (context: Unlike many problems in life, games and puzzles offer agreed goals and tests. In the missionaries-and-cannibals work, researchers sought a human-like pattern of reasoning, not merely an answer; the thing being tested matters.)
- [10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=606s) Hamming: Judge research by what it makes possible as well as its stated target: "it was a failure but it was a good job". (context: The General Problem Solver tried to shrink the distance between a starting state and a goal using general rules plus subject-specific knowledge. Hamming says the ambition failed but produced valuable list-processing methods.)
- [11:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=668s) Hamming: Success in some cases does not establish that you understand when a method works. (context: Expert systems encode rules elicited from doctors and other specialists. Hamming says they have often helped, but their mixture of successes and failures leaves the conditions of success unclear; he does not dismiss every use of them.)
- [12:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=762s) Hamming: Technical capability and permission to deploy it are separate problems: "a doctor is allowed to be human". (context: He argues that diagnostic software could recognize rare diseases and update epidemic probabilities, yet asks who pays when a program errs and who may prescribe its recommended drugs. His claim that it could outperform the average doctor, his liability account, and his prediction of home diagnosis followed by a doctor's prescription are his assessments in this lecture, not established clinical or legal conclusions.)
- [15:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=951s) Hamming: Turn a vague capability question into a constructive one: "can you write a program so the machine can think"? (context: He compares machines extending brain work with power machinery extending muscle work. His challenge concerns producing the behavior with a program, rather than arguing about the machine as an object.)
- [16:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1013s) Hamming: Design for cooperation: "what can man and machine do together that they cannot do separately"? (context: At Bell Labs he resisted his bosses' emphasis on competition between humans and machines. His preferred test was how the combination could help an organization do a better overall job.)
- [17:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1044s) Hamming: Test a definition against concrete borderline cases before relying on it. (context: Someone excluded organic material from machines; Hamming countered with a wooden machine, then a living frog's nervous system used as memory. The examples expose how hard it is to draw a clean boundary between machine and living thing.)
- [19:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1170s) Hamming: Prefer definitions that enable inquiry: "definitions are arbitrary" and should be "fruitful for further action". (context: A Jesuit-trained engineer defined thinking as what humans can do and machines cannot. That settles the dispute by definition but suggests no work for tomorrow; allowing machine thought creates something to investigate. Defining thought as only what Newton and Einstein did has the opposite problem: it excludes nearly everyone in the room.)
- [22:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1325s) Hamming: Question the boundary that reserves thinking for humans. (context: His cat falls, licks its paws, and strolls away as if nothing happened; he reads this as awareness of having made a fool of itself. He also argues that food discrimination and reported training of one-celled animals complicate tying all thinking to a nervous system. These are his interpretations and empirical claims, not demonstrations that animals share human self-consciousness.)
- [24:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1450s) Hamming: A whole can have properties its pieces lack: "an assembly of molecules may have properties that the individual ones do not have". (context: He contrasts his picture of frictionless individual molecules with friction in large assemblies while considering consciousness. The analogy leaves room for collective properties; it does not establish a theory of consciousness.)
- [27:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1635s) Hamming: Do not assume the human mind can grasp every possible thought: "why should you think you can think every thought"? (context: Dogs smell things we cannot, and other animals hear sounds outside our range. He suggests that a brain's construction might likewise limit its thoughts, using our difficulty picturing wave-particle duality as a possible example, not proof of an absolute cognitive limit.)
- [27:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1671s) Hamming: Cultivate "tolerance of ambiguity" instead of forcing every unsettled issue into yes or no. (context: He uses wave-particle duality as an analogy for holding conflicting intuitions about machine thought. He feels self-awareness is real and hard to explain as molecules alone, but explicitly says he may be wrong and has no proof; the physics analogy does not prove his view of mind.)
- [32:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1928s) Hamming: Separate an increasingly successful generalization from proof that it has no exceptions: "after enough successes we jump to the other side". (context: Newton's unification of terrestrial and celestial physics and laboratory synthesis of urea illustrate his account of science abandoning an old divide. He says chemistry moved from only living things making organic compounds to believing anything made by the body could be made in a test tube. His point is the extrapolation beyond tested cases, not that the two views have equal evidence.)
- [32:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=1959s) Hamming: A familiar word is not yet an implementable account of it. (context: He invokes Augustine's difficulty explaining time, then finds he cannot specify what he means by meaning well enough to program it. The obstacle he identifies is his own lack of an operational account, not a demonstration that machines can never deal with meaning.)
- [33:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2021s) Hamming: Check an avowed objection against the dependence you already accept. (context: To the claim that people do not want their lives to depend on machines, he gives pacemakers, hospital monitors, and unstable aircraft stabilized by computers about every millisecond. In the aircraft example the pilot supplies the larger plan while the computer handles corrections too fast for the pilot.)
- [35:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2146s) Hamming: Predictability can make a machine easier to cooperate with than a person: "the machine will operate regularly". (context: He recalls traffic directed by policemen and contrasts it with signals whose changes drivers can anticipate. He also points out that people obey a red light on an empty road even when they judge its instruction unnecessary; actual reliance can differ from professed distrust.)
- [38:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2333s) Hamming: Know where definite rules end and judgment begins: "then there has to be these heuristic rules". (context: In four-by-four-by-four tic-tac-toe, immediate wins, blocks, and forks give clear priorities. After those, favorable positions and forcing moves are plausible guides. An attack that runs out may hand the initiative to the opponent; the attack timing is not supplied by those simple rules. Chess evaluation similarly makes judgments about pieces and positions explicit in a formula.)
- [41:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2492s) Hamming: Use experience to improve a workable starting rule, rather than requiring its author to know the best settings. (context: Samuel perturbs coefficients in a checkers evaluation formula, plays versions against each other, retains improvements, and revisits parameters after reaching a local peak. Hamming illustrates selection with eight wins in ten games; this is a small comparison, not proof of superiority. Random choices between similar moves vary the experience. In his account the program eventually exceeds its author's playing ability.)
- [42:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2555s) Hamming: Apply the same standard to human and machine learning: does being taught how to learn invalidate learning from experience? (context: He recounts a checkers champion changing from amusement to wariness as Samuel's program beats him, then contrasting its weeks of learning with his forty years of play. Against the objection "it was all programmed in", Hamming asks whether a geometry teacher also installs a way to learn. The champion's remark is paraphrased because the captions are garbled; the lecture also varies on which state the champion represented.)
- [44:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2677s) Hamming: Decide what evidence you would accept before defending your verdict on AI. (context: After the checkers example he asks what would count as learning and what would establish an essential human-machine difference beyond personal prejudice or religious conviction. He warns that progress requires struggling with your own beliefs; merely asserting either side supplies no demonstration.)
- [46:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aq_PLEQ9YzI&t=2770s) Hamming: Look for incompatible assumptions in your own practice: "you are enormously ambivalent about whether your child has free will or not". (context: Teachers and parents may act as though the right instruction must produce the desired person, while also treating the learner as freely choosing. He extends this criticism to claims that changing a neighborhood must reform its inhabitants. His target is the implied guarantee of behavior, not evidence that teaching, parenting, or environment has no influence.)
