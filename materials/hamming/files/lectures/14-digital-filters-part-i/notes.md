# Lecture 14 - Digital Filters, Part I · notes
- [02:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=163s) Hamming: Learn new methods before change leaves you behind: "when the world passes you by you are not a happy person". (context: He saw relay experts refuse electronics and analog experts refuse digital computing, becoming both an economic loss and a social loss. He urged Baker to provide books and training before the next transition; Baker turned the responsibility back on him.)
- [03:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=224s) Hamming: Experts sometimes need to "stop and summarize it, write a definitive book", so knowledge is not scattered through 25 papers. (context: Kaiser agreed but wrote nothing. Hamming arranged teaching lunches at least four times a week and wrote the introductory material. The proposed Kaiser-and-Hamming book became Hamming-and-Kaiser, then Hamming alone, with thanks in the preface: "we're still good friends".)
- [05:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=350s) Hamming: Teach unfinished material to debug it before publication: "get some debugging and going through the notes before I turn it into a book". (context: While writing, he offered UCLA Extension a digital-filters course to practice the presentation and work through his notes.)
- [06:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=412s) Hamming: "You don't always do what you want to do you do what you believe needs to be done". Useful work can bring rewards you did not plan. (context: He disliked digital filters and tried to avoid writing the needed book. He reports three editions and courses paying about $5,000 a week in many cities. He says society frequently repays useful work, not that it always does.)
- [07:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=474s) Hamming: Ask "why always the frequency approach" rather than accept an expert's habits. Choose a representation for the operations it makes simple. (context: Kaiser and other engineers did not initially satisfy him. He found that complex exponentials retain their form under translation and linear time-invariant filtering, and a sampled sinusoid aliases to another single sinusoid; a sampled high-degree polynomial has no comparably simple coefficient relation. Linearity alone does not establish the filter eigenfunction claim.)
- [10:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=634s) Hamming: Look for one idea wearing two names: "why have two names for the same thing"? (context: Students had already met eigenfunctions in differential equations and linear algebra, but engineers called the frequency-dependent multiplier a transfer function. Its value at a frequency is the corresponding eigenvalue, not the eigenfunction itself. Engineers he asked said "but we call it the transfer function".)
- [11:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=697s) Hamming: Remove irrelevant scales so different fields share one problem: "microseconds or in years I will have the same equations". (context: He rescales equally spaced samples to times 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on, making an engineer's microseconds and an economist's years comparable.)
- [14:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=852s) Hamming: Understand what an operation destroys: "I cannot find those high frequencies unless I have outside information". (context: Sampling itself makes different frequencies indistinguishable; later processing cannot identify the original high frequency from the samples alone. At the two-samples-per-cycle boundary, a sine can give all zeros, hiding its amplitude. His endpoint warning matters alongside the familiar sampling-rate rule.)
- [17:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1066s) Hamming: When theory needs a quantity nobody can supply, ask a question the people with the problem can answer: "what is the highest frequency I have to worry about"? (context: For a Navy interceptor simulation with 28 differential equations and 25 trajectories, halving the step would double computation. Rather than demand unavailable fifth-derivative bounds, he asked for the relevant frequencies. The engineers accepted responsibility above 10 cycles per second; this gave him a basis for estimating the step and a roughly $25,000 bid.)
- [18:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1128s) Hamming: Turn a plausible estimate into something checked two ways: "lo and behold the two agreed". Match numerical accuracy to the accuracy of the physical inputs. (context: The infinite-sample theorem did not directly set a step for extrapolation from a few past values. He tested a cosine-producing differential equation numerically and derived its behavior on paper: roughly seven samples per cycle were marginal and ten safe for his method and required accuracy. The airplane data did not have six-figure precision. He also compared full runs at different step sizes; these are case-specific checks, not a universal integration rule.)
- [21:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1283s) Hamming: Use physical plausibility to challenge an expensive computation: they were "calculating the wrong problem". (context: A subcontractor used steps of roughly 1/1,000 to 1/10,000 second for a Nike-missile simulation. Its simplified stick model could not support the implied rapid vibrations. He reports that the investigation found a register value displaced by seven bits and roughly a hundredfold excess computation: debugging across a continent without seeing the program. The captions garble the exact multiplier.)
- [23:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1438s) Hamming: A supposed limitation can replace hardware once you understand it: after removing a demodulation rack, "the thing runs better". (context: A colleague first shifted a high-frequency signal down with equipment. For a suitably isolated signal band and chosen sampling rate, aliasing itself could do the shift. Band placement can also reverse frequency order: Bell Labs used this to turn high voice frequencies into low ones and had fun trying music. This depends on avoiding overlap with other signal bands.)
- [26:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1596s) Hamming: Judge an engineering trick by where its savings pay for the extra machinery. (context: He explains sending two voices over one wire by shifting one voice up, separating the bands with filters, and shifting it down at the far end. This made better use of long-distance wires; "it doesn't necessarily pay on local phone calls".)
- [28:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=1722s) Hamming: Do not confuse a familiar fitting criterion with a reason it is appropriate: "I'm not saying it is" a good thing merely because people believe it. Identify what you can actually vary. (context: For a straight-line fit through five observations, the observations and their sample positions are fixed; the adjustable variables are the line's intercept and slope. Centering the positions at -2, -1, 0, 1, 2 cancels the odd sums and makes the fitted midpoint the mean.)
- [33:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=2009s) Hamming: "You never see nature itself it's always through a window"; sometimes the "window is your head". (context: A five-point average and a quadratic fit apply different weights to the same data. He extends that concrete filter window to measuring instruments and habits of thought: "you impose things on what you see because you think that way". Ask how the instrument or mental model shapes the observation.)
- [36:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=2192s) Hamming: Improving one property does not remove every defect: "I cannot avoid those wiggles". (context: For the polynomial-fit smoothing filters he is sketching, fitting higher-degree polynomials makes the frequency response flatter near zero, but oscillations remain elsewhere. A better local fit is not automatically an ideal frequency filter.)
- [38:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=2288s) Hamming: Find a simple decomposition that turns special cases into a general method: "if I can do those two I can do anything". (context: Adding the even and odd parts recovers any function. Applied to the nonrecursive filter coefficients under discussion, this separates the symmetric smoothing part from the antisymmetric differentiating part; mastering those two cases covers the general coefficient pattern.)
- [41:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=2475s) Hamming: Connect a familiar construction to what it optimizes: Fourier coefficients give the least-squares fit, and "this is true of any orthogonal system". (context: He links the orthogonality rules used to compute expansion coefficients to minimizing squared error. This makes Fourier expansion a way to approximate a desired filter response under that criterion; it does not make least squares the right objective for every application.)
- [42:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2wO8ZHnn3Q4&t=2536s) Hamming: Do not let a metaphor restrict the mathematics: "window is a way of speaking". (context: A filter window is a set of weights, zero outside its span. The quadratic smoother includes a negative weight, -3 before normalization. A mathematical window need not behave like a physical pane transmitting only positive amounts; he explicitly says the negative coefficient does not worry him.)
