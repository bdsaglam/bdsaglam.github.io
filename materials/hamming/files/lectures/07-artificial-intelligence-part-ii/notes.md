# Lecture 7 - Artificial Intelligence - Part II · notes
- [01:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=92s) Hamming: Do not infer what a whole can do from what its parts can do: "committees can produce reports that no individual would have ever done". (context: He compares molecules with friction, individuals with mobs and armies, and nonthinking components with intelligence. This is an argument against dismissing emergence, not a proof that a particular machine thinks.)
- [04:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=249s) Hamming: Do not treat size alone as an explanation of intelligence; the organization and program still matter. (context: The brain has specialized structures and many interconnections, not just many identical parts. He questions the claim that a sufficiently big machine will automatically think, while leaving emergence open because "we really do not know what thinking is".)
- [07:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=437s) Hamming: A machine's performance can change a person's attitude from condescension to respect, then humiliation. (context: In his account of a film of Samuel's checkers program beating the Connecticut champion, the champion begins treating it as a real opponent: "that's a good move". Afterward Samuel says it learned with a couple of hours of practice; the man had spent a lifetime. This is Hamming's retelling, not a measured comparison of total human and machine training.)
- [08:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=499s) Hamming: A result can surprise its maker without being independent of prior rules or experience: it was "not put in consciously". Apply that test to human originality too. (context: The geometry program proved equal base angles by comparing triangle ABC with CBA, without adding a line. Its makers did not know the proof, though Hamming says it was already in his copy of Euclid. He calls it "a Nifty proof"; their instruction to try a proof before adding constructions made the surprise possible.)
- [08:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=530s) Hamming: Do not define thinking so that every machine success is disqualified: "the moment the machine does it there's a program and that isn't thinking". (context: He compares programming a machine with a geometry teacher loading procedures into a pupil. If being taught or following rules rules out thinking, the same objection must be faced for humans.)
- [10:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=623s) Hamming: Keep an unresolved question open rather than let your training settle it. He found self-awareness "too real to pretend that they were just imagination". (context: His physics training suggested a world of molecules, yet consciousness troubled that picture. Asking whether you could act otherwise, given exactly who you are and the situation, leads toward determinism; he says there is no way here to prove the proposition.)
- [16:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=967s) Hamming: Use machines to shorten the gap between an idea and hearing what it produces: "the composer can hear the music that he is composing". (context: Composers could otherwise wait years to hear a work outside their imagination. At Bell Labs, a French-horn expert made a tape mixing played and calculated tones that Hamming says could not be told apart; the machine could render supplied notes on simulated instruments.)
- [17:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1060s) Hamming: When a technical constraint is removed, identify the judgment that remains: "what sounds are worth listening to". (context: A conductor can adjust synthesized timing by a millisecond instead of accepting whichever orchestral take has the fewest performance faults. Hamming's sweeping claim that music's technical problems are solved concerns producing sounds, not proving that a machine composes worthwhile music; taste still differs between people.)
- [19:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1152s) Hamming: Separate adapting to a person's taste from creating work that deserves their full attention. He proposes "I like it" and "I don't like it" feedback for a program making background music. (context: Like the checkers learner, the program would adjust from experience. He explicitly limits the proposal to background music, says he is not a musician, and admits that machine-composed music he had heard did not seem very good. Rendering supplied notes is a different achievement.)
- [22:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1342s) Hamming: Do not treat retraining as an automatic answer to displacement: he doubts you can take "coal miners and make them into first class programmers". (context: He recalls a night-shift coffee-and-food attendant and elevator operators whose jobs machines replaced. He says net job counts are too vague to compare and asks what displaced people will do. This is his doubt about a social promise, not evidence that an occupational group cannot learn programming; he says he has no answer.)
- [24:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1495s) Hamming: The best form of an expression depends on the next task: "simplify depends upon what you are planning to do next". (context: Before integrating, split an expression into terms; before finding zeros, gather it into a product. A school committee's rule against radicals in denominators could make an expression look more complicated. His contrast is between autonomous geometry proofs and algebra packages needing human direction, not a claim that symbolic algebra is impossible.)
- [27:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1623s) Hamming: Human direction plus machine bookkeeping can be useful before full autonomy works: "directed stuff the machine is a big big help". (context: In chemical synthesis, a person proposes a route while the program tracks reaction times, ingredient costs, and yields. Changed raw-material prices can make it worth revisiting an old route. He contrasts this with asking for an unguided synthesis and expecting the machine to find it.)
- [29:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1751s) Hamming: Judge automation by how much surprise its setting permits: "when there's no surprise robots are very very good". (context: He contrasts machine-designed chips and robot assembly with the deceptively simple task of putting a nut on a bolt. When the nut sticks, a person tries repairs that are hard to specify in advance. His praise of routine production does not imply equally reliable handling of unexpected cases.)
- [30:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1812s) Hamming: Redesign the method to fit the machine while keeping the intended result: "doing it in essentially a different way which fits the machines better than fits you". (context: On a ship, he suggests robots on ceiling tracks so fallen objects on deck need not block them and rolling need not create the same balancing problem. He says "maybe", not that this design is established as best. It develops his earlier contrast between fixed-wing aircraft and flapping birds.)
- [32:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=1938s) Hamming: Expertise includes choosing a useful view early, not merely trying more possibilities: Shannon had "the gift of looking at the problem right the first time". (context: When colleagues brought Shannon a number-theory problem, he drew a picture while they explained; they saw the answer in it, thanked him, and left. Hamming links this to a chess study in which stronger and weaker players reportedly examined about 100 moves, but only the strongest saw the crucial move. He says the source of this skill is still unknown.)
- [34:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2094s) Hamming: Winning a benchmark can leave the original research question unanswered: "we simply killed the problem by excessive computation". (context: Chess began for him as a way to study human thought; programs examining millions of positions per second outperformed attempts to imitate humans. He contrasts this with players reportedly examining roughly 100 positions, while admitting that their accounts of their own thoughts may be incomplete. Better play does not itself explain expert thinking.)
- [37:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2221s) Hamming: Eventual success is not a practical search method: "you shouldn't hold your breath". (context: Monkeys typing randomly might eventually reproduce the British Museum's books in shelf order. Likewise a random stream might contain the next theory of physics, but one must wait and recognize it. His warning about AI using randomness concerns potentially enormous waiting times; the story does not establish that every randomized method is inefficient.)
- [38:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2283s) Hamming: Notice when your beliefs about human choice change with the case. "We have very very mixed beliefs". (context: Raising children assumes that the right upbringing will shape conduct, yet people also claim free choice. He asks how long a magically repaired New York would stay repaired without changing its people. These are his provocations about conditioning and environment, not evidence that environmental improvements never change behavior.)
- [39:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2378s) Hamming: Question whether thinking is a result, a process, or a degree: "maybe thinking is not what is done it's the way it's done". (context: A child multiplying two three-digit numbers seems to him to be thinking; his own practiced multiplication feels conditioned, and a machine's does not feel like thought. Trying to find the smallest thinking program, below which cutting it would remove thought, also makes him question a yes-or-no definition.)
- [40:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2441s) Hamming: Discussion should make your own thinking clear, not reproduce the teacher's opinion: "my business is you say what you think clearly". (context: Preparing the class debate on whether machines think, he says he does not care which position students take. They must find out what they think and be able to express it.)
- [41:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2473s) Hamming: Look for the next useful application in your own field: "I don't want to hear how it's done in the past". (context: His second discussion question asks how an organization could mechanize further, through desirable new uses. Merely counting its machines does not answer what new work they could enable.)
- [43:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URp-uq3_T3Q&t=2628s) Hamming: Use your own learning history to test claims of complete independence from conditioning. A child in China learns Chinese; one in France learns French: "the environment does control your ability to speak a language". (context: He distinguishes an inborn capacity to learn language from the particular language acquired, and explicitly allows later languages and bilingual households. He uses this to question free will, not to prove strict determinism. His additional claims about apes and bilingual difficulties are his historical assertions, not established here.)
