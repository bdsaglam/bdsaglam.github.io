# Lecture 25 - Creativity · notes
- [00:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=40s) Hamming: Organizations can praise creativity while resisting anyone who departs from the elders’ way. (context: He compares management that knows how things should be done with the demand that people do what their ancestors did; this is his analogy about conformity, not a researched account of all societies or organizations.)
- [02:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=133s) Hamming: Novelty alone does not make a contribution valuable: "not done before doesn't really count". (context: He proposes multiplying two random ten-digit numbers to a friend wanting an original computation. The friend instead wants the biggest known prime and a place in the record book; difficulty or significance matters beyond an unused answer.)
- [03:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=228s) Hamming: Neither ridicule nor wide appreciation establishes an idea’s worth. His "Robert Fulton complex" is the fallacy that because people laughed at a successful inventor, their laughing at you proves you are right. (context: He contrasts neglected artists whose paintings later sell for millions with an important theorem that perhaps only ten people can appreciate. These are his illustrations; he is not offering a test that identifies good ideas in advance.)
- [06:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=417s) Hamming: Recognition and historical credit are unreliable guides to a new idea’s merit. (context: In his telling, continental drift and Mendel’s genetics were neglected; Mendel later received credit, while the early drift writer did not. His dates and discovery history are loose recollections, not checked historical claims.)
- [08:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=514s) Hamming: A familiar method can be a contribution in a field that has not learned to use it: "it's new in their field". (context: He hesitated to sign a magnetics paper because he had only applied least squares. A physicist whose most-requested solid-state paper applied circuit theory told him to sign; Hamming did, though he still had reservations.)
- [09:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=550s) Hamming: What looks easy after the fact may have required crossing a large "psychological distance". (context: For error-correcting codes, the step was choosing mathematics where one plus one is zero. Bringing previously unrelated things together can matter even when the connection is simple once someone points it out.)
- [10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=644s) Hamming: Reading about creativity does not replace experiencing it: "creativity is something like sex". (context: A fourteen-year-old can read every book about sex and still not understand it without involvement—and, he adds, may not understand it even then. His personal discovery stories try to give the listener a vicarious experience rather than a mechanical recipe.)
- [11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=707s) Hamming: Do creative work, then examine it immediately: "when you're up at bat you just swing". Treat the resulting story cautiously. (context: He practiced introspection after creative acts, not while doing them. He cannot explain why the triangular error-correcting code appeared in his mind; at 12:49 he warns that a plausible later rationalization may not be true.)
- [13:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=831s) Hamming: Give a problem sustained attention before expecting an unexpected answer: "you saturate your subconscious with the problem". (context: His method is to make the problem the day’s main emotional concern so it remains available during sleep. This is his account of managing his own thinking, not an established mechanism or a promise that sleep will solve it.)
- [14:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=892s) Hamming: Intense involvement needs an off switch: after reasonable effort, "set it aside and go do something else". (context: He gives no fixed duration. An answer may arrive weeks later, or the problem may need another deliberate attack; pursuing it week after week without respite often fails in his experience.)
- [15:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=922s) Hamming: An unexpected image can suggest a structure without spelling out a solution. (context: He retells Kekulé’s dream of "snakes biting their own tails", rolling like hoops, as the clue to a ring of carbon atoms. This is Hamming’s telling of the dream anecdote, not independently verified discovery history; the useful step is recognizing the connection.)
- [16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=988s) Hamming: Ability alone does not supply the commitment he associates with major work: "those who care greatly are more likely to do something". (context: He recalls a skilled mathematician who went home to play guitar and did very good work, but, in Hamming’s judgment, not the very best he could have done. This is his personal assessment of emotional involvement, not evidence that outside interests prevent great work.)
- [17:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1021s) Hamming: A false solution can still narrow the search: "go back to the drawing board". (context: An answer that initially seems right may fail on further work. Returning to the drawing board with a clearer picture of what cannot work is progress, even though the solution itself must be discarded.)
- [17:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1052s) Hamming: When stuck, ask "what would an answer look like if I had it" and which information it really depends on. (context: Conservation of energy or momentum constrains possible answers. His question about Earth’s position and Jupiter’s satellites leads to the light-travel-time example: a seemingly peripheral factor may matter to observed timing, so test relevance rather than simply deleting it.)
- [18:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1114s) Hamming: Rebuild a private discovery into an explanation others can use; stripping away scaffolding can improve your own understanding. (context: He says Maxwell found his equations through a mechanical model of gear trains, then removed the model. His proof example asks "where in the proof did I use continuity"; if it was not used, the assumption can go.)
- [19:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1178s) Hamming: An analogy need only suggest the next move; it need not match every detail. (context: Maxwell’s gear trains and the snake-ring story illustrate what he calls a "very very loose and weak" analogy. An exact match would merely reproduce the old thing; notice differences as well as the resemblance.)
- [21:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1301s) Hamming: Build several ways to recall an idea while learning it: put "little hooks on the idea". (context: Tukey repeatedly connected radar to polarized light that Hamming also knew but failed to retrieve. Hamming began turning new knowledge over in other settings; at 22:12 he likens the result to being able to "pull up the fish the other guy misses" and recalls connecting Gibbs phenomena to diffraction and electron microscopy.)
- [24:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1489s) Hamming: Seek people who stimulate thought, including difficult people, and explain your ideas to them. (context: Explaining forced him to clean up confusion; a useful reply supplied an analogy or earlier case rather than "that's interesting". Some of his most stimulating colleagues were awkward to get along with. He contrasts this with scheduled brainstorming, whose ineffectiveness is his assessment here, not a demonstrated universal result.)
- [28:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1707s) Hamming: Change a small mental habit first; a teacher cannot do the practice for you. (context: He compares creativity training with running a four-minute mile, and mental habits with dieting or quitting smoking. People who want to "lose 10 pounds the first five minutes" get discouraged; small changes build the ability to change larger patterns.)
- [29:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1740s) Hamming: Turn the person you want to become into choices you make now: "don't tell lies". (context: He catches himself behaving against his own aims and tries to stop that behavior. The same responsibility applies to changing how he thinks; merely listening to a teacher cannot make the change for him.)
- [29:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1772s) Hamming: Learning to drop a problem protects the rest of a career. (context: He calls Einstein’s later unified-field search "the wrong problem at the wrong time and the wrong way", while allowing that a unified theory might still be possible. His imagined request that Einstein work on something else for six months or a year is a proposed intervention, not an event; his verdict on Einstein’s later work is his own.)
- [32:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=1928s) Hamming: A solution that keeps receding by the same amount is a warning to reconsider: "it's not a guarantee but it's a suggestion". (context: A nonlinear-filter researcher still expected results in eighteen months after a year’s work. Dropping too soon also risks missing success. His Washington example—"had he lost Revolution he would have been stubborn"—shows how outcomes color the distinction between persistence and stubbornness; Hamming was content when others later solved problems his circumstances had not let him solve.)
- [33:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=2021s) Hamming: Do not postpone creative work until conditions feel ideal: "what you think are ideal are not". (context: He provocatively says the Institute for Advanced Study "has killed more good people than any place else has ever created", contrasting comfortable appointments with work under pressure. This is his polemical judgment, not a measured comparison or proof that bad conditions improve everyone’s work.)
- [34:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=2053s) Hamming: Manage yourself through traits you actually have: his "cornered rat Theory" turned pride and self-confidence into effort. (context: He promised a Monday answer before knowing how to find it; reluctance to admit failure then drove weekend work. He saw this as converting character defects into an asset, but explicitly says it may not work for others, who need methods that fit them.)
- [37:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=2243s) Hamming: Use a new environment to escape the behavior others expect from you. (context: On the train to graduate school in Nebraska he decided to give up the "snotty quick answer". He had many acquaintances but no close friends: a joke could be funny and still hurt someone enough to drive them away. Where nobody knew him, nobody expected the old performance.)
- [39:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=2399s) Hamming: Build a useful reputation around your strengths: "what kind of things you want to be known able to do"? (context: Bell Labs colleagues sought him for difficult integrals, giving him practice; he knew whom to ask about Bessel functions. His later reputation, as he understood it, was making progress when nobody knew what to do. Choose worthwhile work you can actually do and let repeated contributions establish it.)
- [43:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlTybZvds0U&t=2618s) Hamming: Reconsider how you contribute as a career develops; more knowledge and technique do not automatically mean more originality. (context: A Bell Labs historical chart credited work he was closely associated with only in his first fifteen years, none in his last fifteen; he tore the admired chart off his door and threw it away. He became a teacher, like an athlete becoming a coach. His claim that mathematics favors youth—including "you're 40 forget" at 42:36—is his broad judgment, with late exceptions and other fields acknowledged, not a proven age cutoff or a finding about the listener’s field.)
