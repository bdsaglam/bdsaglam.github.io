# Lecture 30 - You and Your Research · notes
- [00:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=33s) Hamming: Study how capable people work instead of treating your present role as your ceiling. He felt like a "janitor of science" and was "envious — plain envious". (context: At Los Alamos he kept work going but did not participate in major decisions. Asking what separated him from leading scientists began the study he continued at Bell Labs.)
- [01:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=103s) Hamming: Recognition can compound into access to knowledge: "Once famous, it's very easy remain famous". (context: His Matthew Effect example is being invited to IBM, then shown laboratories and production lines that less famous people did not see. His warning that the unrecognized have their work taken away is his broad characterization of scientific credit, not a demonstrated universal rule.)
- [02:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=139s) Hamming: Choose significant work by your own definition of significance. His stories aim to provoke "Well, if Hamming can do it, why couldn't I?" (context: With only one life as far as he knows, merely getting by does not satisfy him. At 02:51 he describes his deliberately uncomfortable encouragement as sticking a knife in your back and giving it a good twist; he is asking for ambition, not prescribing his goals as yours.)
- [05:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=305s) Hamming: Prepare daily so an opportunity can become a contribution: "when the lightning strikes, you're either ready or you're not". (context: Information theory was in the air around Shannon and Hamming, but Shannon had already connected Boolean algebra to switching circuits. Hamming argues that repeated contributions by the same people cannot be explained by luck alone; he explicitly concedes an element of luck.)
- [06:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=373s) Hamming: Sustained engagement matters beyond an isolated flash of inspiration. (context: He completes Edison's saying, "Genius is 99% perspiration and 1% inspiration", begun at 05:42, and recalls Los Alamos scientists talking shop even on Sunday hikes. These are an attributed maxim and his observation, not measured percentages; at 27:42 he insists that working hardest alone does not win.)
- [08:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=508s) Hamming: Judge the promise of an idea rather than its author's local reputation: "A prophet is without honor in his own country". (context: Bill Pfann's department thought little of him, but Hamming saw that zone melting could purify transistor materials. He supplied computing instruction and machine time for repeated passes whose end effects made hand calculation impractical. Pfann later gained confidence and spoke well; the story challenges early judgments of ability, not the value of mathematical skill.)
- [10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=644s) Hamming: Have enough confidence to attempt an unconventional attack. (context: Shannon averaged over random codes to show that at least one good code must exist; Hamming sees courage in even trying that approach. He immediately allows that confidence can become overconfidence, so belief is permission to investigate rather than evidence that an idea is correct.)
- [11:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=709s) Hamming: Borrow a useful habit from someone you admire, then use it when stuck: "I ain't scared of nothing". (context: Shannon would attack back in chess, advance his queen into a complicated position, and force the game to resolve into a win or loss. Hamming deliberately repeated the phrase to himself and sometimes broke through; the anecdote includes losing, not guaranteed success.)
- [12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=740s) Hamming: Choose everyday company from whom you can learn a working style. (context: He left a mathematics lunch group occupied with games for the physics table, where he watched Shockley and others work and learned their tricks. When promotions and departures changed that table, he moved again; the point is to seek learning rather than preserve a habit.)
- [13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=811s) Hamming: Ask whether your present work can lead to something important: "if what you're working on is not important and it's not likely to lead to important things, why are you working on it?" (context: After this challenge to the chemistry table he ate with the engineers. Dave later said he had spent the summer thinking about important problems without changing his research; Hamming reports his later promotion and academy membership, an anecdote rather than proof that the question caused his success.)
- [15:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=916s) Hamming: Do not demand that every starting problem already look grand: "plant little acorns which grow into mighty oak trees". (context: He thinks some Nobel winners, including Shannon in this comparison, stop producing because they accept only visibly important work. Choose small problems with growth potential; at 15:50 he adds that committees and promotions also consume the time fame appears to free. These are his judgments about careers.)
- [16:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=984s) Hamming: Persistence requires judgment in both directions: he has seen people "abandon a good idea too soon" and "cling to a bad idea too long". (context: Confidence versus overconfidence resembles being strong-willed versus stubborn. He offers no mechanical test for the boundary; the desire to do excellent work is a steadier guide than attachment to one idea.)
- [16:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1018s) Hamming: Reserve care for work that deserves it rather than polishing every obligation. (context: He rejects his father's "anything worth doing at all is worth doing well" as an absolute: signing routine papers should simply be disposed of. Excellence gives direction to substantive work without requiring beautiful handwriting on every form.)
- [17:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1053s) Hamming: A direction lets effort accumulate instead of canceling itself. (context: His drunken sailor travels a distance proportional to the square root of the number of random steps; with a pretty girl as a destination, the distance becomes proportional to the number of steps. This is his random-walk analogy for a vision of excellence, not a numerical prediction of career output.)
- [18:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1126s) Hamming: Protect contact with reality as well as time to concentrate: "I can only establish the correlation". (context: In his Bell Labs observations, people with closed doors could work hard for ten years yet choose slightly wrong problems; open doors admitted interruptions and knowledge of what mattered. He explicitly cannot tell whether an open door causes an open mind or the reverse.)
- [19:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1190s) Hamming: Comfort and freedom can trap you in elaborating your first success. (context: He thinks the Institute for Advanced Study "sterilized" many able researchers by insulating them from life; von Neumann is his counterexample, traveling and remaining receptive to new ideas. This is Hamming's stated opinion about the institute, not an independently checked verdict on its researchers.)
- [21:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1294s) Hamming: Turn a missing resource into a different formulation: "why don't you make the machine do the programming?" (context: Bell Labs would not give him the "acre of girls" used by airframe companies for programming, but leaving would sacrifice access to many people he wanted to learn from. Automating programming put him at a frontier: "What appeared to be a defect, by turning the problem around, became an asset". His description reflects the staffing and language of his anecdote.)
- [23:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1425s) Hamming: Find what the work must establish, beyond merely producing answers: "Both are equally effective, but one was defensible and one was not". (context: His aircraft-interception simulation used an ugly patched integration method. Expecting analog-computer advocates to scrutinize the military report, he realized he was demonstrating digital computing on their home ground. He derived a cleaner method and checked a trajectory against the old answers; he explicitly does not recommend his then-suitable method for current machines.)
- [24:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1496s) Hamming: Study successful work to discover techniques you can adapt: "You can't be everybody, but you have to find your own method". (context: Reflecting on a computing result valuable to transistor research, he urges studying both your successes and those of Galileo, Newton, and others. His deliberately forceful "You don't study your failures, study successes" argues for learning how success happens; this passage does not give a qualified rule about when failure analysis is useful.)
- [26:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1566s) Hamming: Replace envy of another person's knowledge with a concrete change in your learning habits. (context: Asked how Tukey could know so much at the same age, their boss replied, "you'd be surprised how much you'd know if you worked as hard as he did". Hamming admits he lacked Tukey's energy but could work harder. He became a book-review editor to keep relevant books within reach, then revisited each review after a week to test whether it represented the book.)
- [27:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1662s) Hamming: Effort needs judgment: "The person who works on the right problem at the right time in the right way is what counts". (context: After explaining the leisure reading he sacrificed, he says the hardest worker does not necessarily win. His encouragement at 28:14 is that there are "a million races being run": find a consequential opportunity suited to your approach rather than merely maximize hours.)
- [29:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1760s) Hamming: Make a regular appointment to question your direction: "10 percent of my time" went to important problems. (context: His Friday noon and afternoon "great thoughts" asked how computing would affect science and AT&T, and what software was. He preferred Friday because the questions could linger through the weekend, whereas a Monday meeting would break them up; routine calls and signatures were still allowed.)
- [30:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1823s) Hamming: Keep revisiting fundamentals while learning selectively. (context: Fundamentals are the things other things seem to depend on and that might remain true tomorrow, not facts guaranteed never to change. New fields keep appearing; he asks whether each is relevant to his own work because he cannot learn them all.)
- [30:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1853s) Hamming: Tolerate enough ambiguity to "both believe and disbelieve". (context: Belief in a theory sustains work; disbelief lets you notice contradictions and move to a new theory rather than only elaborate the old one. He says it took fifteen or twenty years of studying great people to notice this trait and admits he has no idea how to teach a course in it.)
- [32:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1920s) Hamming: Keep unsolved important questions ready for a clue. (context: He says great scientists often carry "10 to 20 problems" they cannot yet attack, then drop other work when a clue arrives. This is his estimate and strategy, not a prescribed count or a claim that all of these problems should be actively pursued at once.)
- [32:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=1953s) Hamming: Judge a research problem by an available attack as well as its possible consequences: "importance is not the consequences". (context: Time travel, teleportation, and antigravity would have enormous economic effects, yet he says nobody at Bell Labs worked on them because there was no way in. In this advice, an idea for how to proceed turns a consequential wish into a research opportunity.)
- [34:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2050s) Hamming: Rework a discovery until another person can understand it clearly. (context: He imagines lying in bed trying different explanations for Joe and Jill: the clear account often differs from the route of discovery. His preceding claim that Poincaré and others had special relativity before Einstein is his historical characterization, not a settled attribution; the practical lesson is to make the underlying idea straightforward.)
- [34:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2084s) Hamming: Learn formal speaking, written reports, and immediate conversation. (context: In a conference you must be able to explain an objection and its reasons while a decision is being made; a report written tomorrow may arrive too late. His three levels of communication make an idea usable at the moment it matters.)
- [35:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2119s) Hamming: Study why talks work and adapt only what you can perform. (context: His after-dinner pattern puts a joke at the beginning, one in the middle to wake listeners, and one at the end to be remembered. He can tell one-liners but not shaggy-dog stories. Critiquing other talks and comparing impressions with friends builds a basis for judging his own, rather than mechanically following a book.)
- [36:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2217s) Hamming: Do the best work the actual circumstances allow. (context: He calls it "a poor workman who blames his tools". Broadcasting intimidated this class into withholding questions; after finding he could not get much interaction, he adapted his teaching rather than waiting for an ideal class. This example admits a real loss from the conditions.)
- [38:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2284s) Hamming: "Change does not mean progress, but progress requires change". (context: His provocative rule was to try another way if a department had done something the same way for ten years, even satisfactorily. Some trials will make things worse, he explicitly admits; avoiding every change also prevents finding better methods.)
- [39:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2353s) Hamming: Make knowledge retrievable by connecting it from several directions: "put hooks on ideas". (context: Tukey could retrieve information Hamming recognized as true only after hearing it. Hamming began turning each new fact around and relating it to many other facts so that different situations could bring it to mind.)
- [40:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2415s) Hamming: Demonstrate ability before expecting broad freedom. (context: A Nebraska department head told an instructor seeking less teaching to do the research first; Hamming did error-correcting codes at home. Later managers left him alone because he would worry about what should be done. He says that trust suited his compulsive conscientiousness and would not suit everyone; this is his experience and advice, not a guarantee of institutional reward.)
- [41:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2478s) Hamming: First-class achievement can be worth the struggle, but his evidence comes from those who succeeded. (context: Famous people he asked called it "better than wine, women, and song put together". He did not ask women, jokes that they might reverse the phrase, and explicitly admits he was afraid to ask those who tried and failed. Keep that selection limit beside his enthusiastic verdict.)
- [42:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw&t=2545s) Hamming: Examine your life so its direction reflects what you consider important. (context: After recalling a Yale professor repeating "the unexamined life is not worth living", he asks whether merely having a good time will satisfy you in old age. At 43:02 he calls himself a "revivalist preacher": his final appeal is to choose a life and a working style deliberately, not to accept whatever happens.)
