# Lecture 20 - Simulation, Part III · notes
- [02:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=159s) Hamming: Use a quick qualitative picture to challenge a model before investing in calculation: "a simple sketch will often reveal an enormous amount". (context: At lunch with physicists he drew direction fields on placemats. If all solutions ran to infinity when the phenomenon could not, either the equation or their understanding needed correction.)
- [05:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=314s) Hamming: Spend computation where the problem changes quickly; "you don't use the same step size all the way along". (context: He compares predictor and corrector to choose a step size. A moon flight can, in his example, take steps of roughly an hour in smooth mid-flight gravity, but needs small steps near Earth and the moon. Agreement estimates local error; the later convergence/divergence example shows why it does not guarantee accuracy everywhere.)
- [08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=500s) Hamming: Judge accuracy where you need the answer: "garbage in good answers out" can coexist with good input producing bad intermediate answers. (context: In his Gaussian-shaped solution family, nearby trajectories separate toward the middle and converge afterward. An accurate endpoint does not certify the midpoint; this challenges the blanket GIGO slogan, not the need to examine input quality.)
- [08:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=531s) Hamming: Do not trust a low-dimensional mental picture when judging a large system's errors: "those tubes you're drawing are not what you think". (context: For the 28-variable Navy intercept problem, he warns against simply imagining a narrow tube around the solution. He recalls the earlier four-circle example whose high-dimensional geometry defeats the familiar picture; each variable still needs prediction, correction, and error checking.)
- [11:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=693s) Hamming: When borrowing a method, check the extra feedback paths in its new setting. (context: A differential-equation corrector resembles a recursive digital filter, but its computed values also pass through the differential equation to produce new derivatives, possibly nonlinearly. Stability is therefore more complicated than treating those derivatives as independent input signals.)
- [13:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=787s) Hamming: Choose what an approximation must preserve from the simulation's purpose: "do we want the solution to be accurate or do we want it to feel right"? (context: For a Mars landing trainer he favors realistic motion over slightly better position accuracy: the pilot can steer, but unexpected acceleration or aliased high-frequency motion changes the experience. He calls his drawn corners an exaggeration, cannot reproduce lower gravity indefinitely, and admits they do not know enough about which frequencies pilots sense.)
- [15:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=941s) Hamming: Choose a representation that explains the physics, rather than judging methods only by their arithmetic cost: "it tells me more about the physics". (context: He favors fitting frequency response because it communicates oscillation and stability to physicists; saying a solution is not locally polynomial tells them less. Frequency-based and polynomial-based formulas can use essentially the same arithmetic with different coefficients. His preference is not proof that polynomials are always the wrong approximation.)
- [20:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1251s) Hamming: A useful simulation needs equations containing the relevant mechanism: "it wasn't the computation". (context: The first two Nike test missiles shook apart, stopping the planned data collection. A woman using a desk calculator produced pitch and yaw curves that alternately transferred growing energy. Engineers immediately recognized the neglected cross connection; one hit his head against the wall in anger. The transfer period agreed with damaged telemetry. The equations contained the coupling even though their earlier reasoning had missed it.)
- [21:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1282s) Hamming: Imperfect starting data can suffice when the system removes those errors: "the guidance system would bring them right back on the path". (context: He could not afford to hand-calculate the entire missile flight, so they guessed seven initial conditions near the disaster. Guidance corrected small trajectory deviations: a convergent direction field made those particular starting errors tolerable, even while coupled pitch and yaw could become unstable.)
- [22:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1376s) Hamming: Separate the precision a computation needs from the accuracy of its underlying data, and ask which features actually drive the result. (context: At Los Alamos, a plausible curve through sparse equation-of-state estimates was read to three and a half figures and tabulated to five or six, though he reckons the curve was good to only "about one and a half". He stayed six extra months partly to understand the successful predictions: nearby-value second differences needed many digits, while local curvature errors approximately compensated as shells traversed the curve. This is his explanation of this case, not a license to manufacture accurate data by adding digits.)
- [25:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1537s) Hamming: Build tolerance of inaccurate components into the design, rather than leaving protective principles "grafted on the end". (context: In Harold Black's negative-feedback amplifier, an accurate feedback resistor can determine the result despite drift in a high-gain amplifier. Hamming connects this with error-correcting codes: most parts need not be individually accurate for the system to work accurately. He wants these principles incorporated into design methods; protection still depends on the arrangement and the critical components.)
- [29:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1763s) Hamming: Use the way a method fails as information for a better method: "this very instability is the essence of the problem". (context: His transistor equation was unstable in either integration direction, like "walking on the crest of a sand dune". Attempts to reject the infinity boundary or the equation failed on physical grounds. After days of thought, he tried slopes, used upward or downward escape to tell which way to adjust, and followed the solution piece by piece. His challenge is not to give up merely because familiar methods fail.)
- [31:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=1918s) Hamming: An interpretation can reveal the observer more than the object: "no you got a bunch of dirty pictures". (context: In his inkblot joke, a boy sees sexual scenes in random blots; when the psychologist calls his mind dirty, the boy blames the pictures. Like seeing animals in clouds, it illustrates projecting meaning onto ambiguous material. Hamming's belief that the Rorschach test had been abandoned is his aside, not a checked claim about clinical practice.)
- [34:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2042s) Hamming: Be willing to "replace a theory by no Theory", rather than demand a replacement story. (context: His psychologist friend gave participants about twelve switches, red and green lights, and twenty trials each; each passed a theory to the next person. The lights were actually random, yet none spotted it. Hamming says a check confirmed his guess that none was a statistician or information theorist. His lesson is the neglected possibility of no signal, not proof that these professions are immune to false patterns.)
- [34:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2074s) Hamming: Statistical judgments can be wrong in either direction, and depend on assumptions: "provided all the rest of the assumptions were correct". (context: He distinguishes finding a signal that is absent from missing one that is present, then says going from 90% to 95% confidence requires more experiments. His "nine out of 10 times you'll be right" is a simplified description here, not a general guarantee that a test's total error rate or a particular conclusion's probability of truth equals its stated significance or confidence level.)
- [36:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2204s) Hamming: Repeatedly revising a simulation can improve it or merely tune it to your expectations: "did he fix the coefficient until he got what he wanted"? (context: After quoting Jay Forrester on moving between assumptions and whole-system behavior, Hamming asks how to distinguish learning from an inkblot interpretation. He explicitly says Forrester is a good man who probably did not do this; he warns about the method's vulnerability in less careful hands, rather than establishing misconduct.)
- [37:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2266s) Hamming: Good intentions do not prevent biased observation: researchers with "the best intention of the world could not avoid seeing what they wanted to see". (context: His medical example motivates hiding treatment assignments from both patients and observers until data collection ends. He immediately warns that blinding can fail: patients sharing reports of headache and diarrhea on a bulletin board may infer who received treatment. The anecdote illustrates why safeguards themselves need scrutiny; it is not evidence that every blinded trial fails.)
- [40:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2423s) Hamming: Inspect the mental filter as well as the measuring instrument: "we constantly see our own Footprints". (context: Like a digital filter's window, preconceived notions shape what becomes evidence. He describes earlier experimenters recording an effect but dismissing it as bad data, leaving someone later to recognize it. He gives no named case here; the point is to examine an anomaly before automatically discarding it.)
- [41:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2518s) Hamming: Revisit old impossibility judgments when capabilities change: "I now had a better computer" changed what was possible. (context: He recalls dismissing Fourier-transform calculations after a better computer had made them feasible. An accepted judgment may no longer fit changed conditions. His prediction of faster future change is his outlook, not a measured forecast.)
- [42:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2578s) Hamming: Actively test your own desire to find a pattern: "am I finding what I want"? (context: Returning to the random-light experiment, he compares invented rules with the superstition that bad luck comes in threes. He allows that he cannot rule out every claimed pattern, but warns that self-deception is harder to expose when effects are small. Seeing a goat in a cloud does not establish that a goat is there.)
- [44:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2673s) Hamming: Do not confuse your imagined behavior in someone else's position with evidence of theirs. (context: His older brother said "everybody in the world is a liar". Hamming eventually interpreted this as projection: his brother imagined situations in which he himself would lie. The spouse example cuts both ways: expecting dishonesty can create suspicions, while assuming complete honesty can hide lies. These are his illustrations of interpretation, not diagnoses of every disagreement.)
- [46:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2766s) Hamming: A shared feedback principle does not remove the need to understand the whole system: "I could never give you a decent answer". (context: After years examining program and differential-analyzer flow diagrams, he still could not reliably judge which interacting positive and negative feedback loops dominated. He returns to the Gaussian example: enough convergence can restore the endpoint after a badly inaccurate middle. This is his admission of difficulty, not a claim that system stability is impossible to analyze.)
- [47:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uf-wC8OkJTM&t=2859s) Hamming: A leader must protect truth-seeking from the pressure to deliver a pleasing answer: "not what he really wanted which was the truth". (context: He warns that junior officers are sensitive to senior officers' wishes and can supply the result both parties hoped for. Having heard the simulation warnings, the senior officer must scrutinize the work rather than reward agreement with the desired conclusion.)
