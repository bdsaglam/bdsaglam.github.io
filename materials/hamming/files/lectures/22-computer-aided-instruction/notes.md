# Lecture 22 - Computer Aided Instruction · notes
- [01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=60s) Hamming: Past failures of effortless learning warrant skepticism, but do not prove a new tool cannot help: there is "no Royal Road to Geometry". (context: He compares learning with running a four-minute mile: money and coaching help only so much. Sleep learning, Dianetics, and speed-reading promises have not convinced him, but combining computers with normal learning remains a legitimate question; he allows that meditation may bring happiness without the worldly success he is testing.)
- [04:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=278s) Hamming: Separate the effect of a teaching method from the attention and expectation surrounding its trial: "the method May in fact be worse". (context: In his Hawthorne story, productivity rises after walls and lighting change, and again when original conditions return. He says excited teachers and attentive students can similarly make a new method look better, and invokes double-blind experiments. His sweeping criticism of educational studies is his claim, not an independent review of them.)
- [06:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=401s) Hamming: Motivation can itself be part of teaching: he tentatively favors a method "which is constantly changing". (context: He reasons that novelty can keep both professor and students expecting improvement, even when it does not establish the superiority of the particular method. This is his conjecture from the Hawthorne effect, not a demonstrated universal teaching rule.)
- [09:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=557s) Hamming: An attractive labor-saving program is not a success unless people keep using it. (context: The grader generates admissible random inputs, compares a student program with the professor's solution within specified tolerances, and records results. On returning about three years later, he finds it abandoned after a monitor-system change required small repairs; he reports similar cases elsewhere. The stated maintenance problem is observed; his further explanation that machine education lacks something is an interpretation.)
- [11:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=681s) Hamming: Ask what an efficient replacement leaves out: education includes learning to live and work with people who think differently. (context: After imagining films and extensive calculus backup material, he asks whether four years without a professor would count as an education. He recalls two gifted, home-educated mathematicians with poor human relations; their upbringing as the cause is his anecdotal interpretation, not a general result about home education.)
- [13:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=805s) Hamming: Reach and popularity do not establish learning: ask "do the students learn more better faster" and specify the claimed improvement. (context: PLATO connects students in Scotland, Canada, and Kentucky; he replies that the telephone company can connect them. Asked whether the claimed 10% gain is a fixed lead or a saving in each course, accumulating to roughly two years over 24 years, its advocate cannot say. Hamming reports receiving no convincing evidence; this is not a finding that PLATO had no benefits.)
- [14:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=869s) Hamming: A good teaching idea can be lost between its inventor and the lesson the student receives. (context: At Irvine he sees a physics-education project whose professor raises money and manages while less imaginative colleagues write the actual material. His dismissive label is "the Stooges"; the lesson is to inspect the delivered teaching rather than infer its quality from the project leader.)
- [16:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=962s) Hamming: Adaptive branching must preserve the ability to retrace and browse: "it's bound is a book but it's not a book". (context: A programmed text sends readers to different pages according to their answers, making yesterday's passage hard to find because they may not remember the branch path. He explicitly allows that a computer can record that path and mitigate this weakness.)
- [16:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=992s) Hamming: Seek expertise before deciding outside your knowledge, then ask for evidence rather than persuasive promises. (context: Reviewing a programmed-learning book for ACM, he consults a Bell Labs social scientist and attends a conference. The expert predicts "no one will ever produce any real evidence"; Hamming says the talks supply claims rather than experiments allowing for the Hawthorne effect. He rejects the book but retains "I'm not sure of course" about his retrospective judgment. His backyard-waterfall pump story illustrates the same value of having an expert nearby.)
- [18:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1086s) Hamming: Observe what users mean by their actions: a wrong answer can be curiosity rather than ignorance. (context: He reports good students deliberately picking a stupid answer "out of boredom" to discover the lesson's response. Their wandering undermines the simple prediction that better students always finish branching lessons faster; his report does not establish that every adaptive system must fail.)
- [19:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1147s) Hamming: Use the learner's particular errors to choose practice rather than repeat the same drill for everyone. (context: A multiplication tutor could notice trouble with sevens or with the order of larger and smaller digits, then generate targeted problems. He thinks intelligent rote-learning programs can help, but explicitly adds that adult or parental reinforcement may be more effective than the machine's response.)
- [20:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1209s) Hamming: Machines can provide safe, varied practice until an urgent response no longer requires conscious thought. (context: Pilot trainers rehearse dangerous situations without risking a life or plane, realistically enough that "the pilots come out sweating". His childhood fencing required an immediate parry "without benefit of thought"; that does not eliminate an overall plan. Driving and a neighbor's baseball-pitching machine make the same distinction between planning and practiced response.)
- [23:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1392s) Hamming: Judge an exercise by the capacity it develops, not just the total work completed: "am I cutting the weights in half"? (context: He proposes replacing one 250-pound graduation lift with two 125-pound lifts, then asks whether an easier mathematical proof similarly weakens the exercise. The point is developing "mental muscles", not moving weights. He explicitly rejects bad lecturing as the conclusion and later asks how far the physical analogy is reasonable.)
- [25:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1516s) Hamming: A brilliant explanation can hide the difficulties you must learn to handle alone: is it "educating you or is it entertaining you"? (context: Fermi reportedly uses a log of infinity of 20 and later 10 without explaining the switch. Metropolis leaves his lucid lecture with a blank note sheet, then falls into the holes Fermi had avoided when reconstructing it. Hamming also criticizes Feynman in this spirit; these are his judgments, not a claim that clear teaching is inherently bad.)
- [26:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1577s) Hamming: Independent mastery supports original work: "that which you learn from others you can use to follow"; "that which you learn for yourself you can use to lead". (context: He says conversations with people who made major creative contributions repeatedly traced their achievement to something studied independently and deeply. He offers this as a rule drawn from those conversations, not a proof that learning from others precludes leadership.)
- [27:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1642s) Hamming: Self-paced completion can miss an educational goal if learning promptly matters to the work. (context: Arguing with the extension dean, he says "you don't want the chief of staff to be a slow learner". He regards fairly rapid learning and use of new knowledge as part of preparation for responsibility, while explicitly saying a leader need not be the fastest at everything.)
- [28:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1704s) Hamming: Define the education you want before claiming a machine delivers it; today's definition may merely flatter its author. (context: He jokes that any definition of the educated person will turn out to classify its proposer as educated. In 1995 he wants to prepare students for an unknown 2020, anticipating book-sized laptops with a term's textbooks on one disk; access to facts and methods then challenges what a lecture should accomplish.)
- [31:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=1894s) Hamming: If automation removes an exercise, replace the deeper ability it trained: "you cannot memorize your way through". (context: Analytic integration makes students recognize an underlying pattern in "Thousand disguises". A friend could perform the derivative procedure without understanding it; Hamming deliberately varies integrals to force recognition instead. Integration software can finish the task sooner, but he asks where else the student will lift the equivalent 250-pound weight. The spoken board formulas are garbled in captions, so no exact formula is reconstructed here.)
- [33:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2024s) Hamming: Before removing apparently needless material, trace what later skills depend on it. (context: He recalls a Chicago school teaching reading by syllables while omitting the alphabet. Reading initially succeeds; late-high-school students then struggle to navigate dictionaries and telephone directories, whose arbitrary ordering requires automatic alphabet knowledge. He calls the change "a Lulu". The episode and his claim that older pupils could not sufficiently overlearn the alphabet remain his account, not independently established outcomes.)
- [36:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2181s) Hamming: Training on one shared strategy can teach predictable behavior that an opponent exploits. (context: He says business and war games can fail when everyone learns the same responses. As a boy he beat his brother at blindfold chess while watching girls on the beach, until his brother made "irregular ridiculous moves" and he lost track. He qualifies that more practice might have let him cope; the story exposes his dependence on familiar patterns, not the impossibility of tracking unusual moves.)
- [38:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2335s) Hamming: Prepare people for the world in which they will make decisions, rather than merely reproduce your own education. (context: Contrasting a Latin-heavy English education with modern technical schooling, he predicts that appropriate education in 2020 may differ just as much again. He identifies a hole in his own preparation: "the human factors and legal factors" often controlled engineering decisions. This is a forecast from 1995 and an interpretation of his experience.)
- [40:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2427s) Hamming: Technical feasibility does not settle how people will use a system; human behavior belongs in engineering education. (context: In his picture-phone pilot story, executives can switch between seeing the caller and seeing their own outgoing image. After three or four months, almost all reportedly choose their own image: "we didn't know humans enough". He uses this to explain why an apparently attractive project could disappoint; the anecdote does not by itself establish every cause of the commercial failure.)
- [42:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2550s) Hamming: Distinguish training a response from educating judgment when evaluating computer-aided instruction. (context: He expects computers to help training but doubts their value for education, even predicting harm. Admirals he has hosted describe the school as 60% education and 40% training, or 70% and 30%; these are their estimates as he reports them. He presents his own course as an attempt to educate through how things actually worked, while admitting he cannot define the educated person or confidently say how to produce one.)
- [45:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA3-mLhajkk&t=2705s) Hamming: Check an attractive project against actual use, including your own mistaken expectations: "I was flatly wrong". (context: He admires Nike's elaborate analog simulator for plugging in real fins and gyros while simulating the rest, and regularly uses its parts for other military calculations. Only afterward does he realize he never saw a component brought in for the intended tests; managers give different excuses. He concludes that "technical people don't behave rationally according to their own criteria", an anecdotal warning to study engineers' behavior rather than assume technical intent guarantees use.)
