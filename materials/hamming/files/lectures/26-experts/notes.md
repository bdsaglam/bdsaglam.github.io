# Lecture 26 - Experts · notes
- [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=0s) Hamming: Expert authority can win an argument without answering it: an expert is "one who knows everything about nothing". (context: After the joke about a briefcase and being 50 miles from home, he describes specialists overwhelming a generalist with jargon and impressive basic principles that may be irrelevant. He has played both roles; the warning concerns how authority is used, not whether specialists know anything.)
- [01:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=97s) Hamming: Education passes on an unspoken way of seeing, not just formulas: "the style is not mentioned it's just delivered". (context: His account of Kuhn's paradigm includes which questions students learn to ask and which answers count. Relativity and quantum mechanics changed the framework itself, opening different kinds of questions.)
- [02:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=129s) Hamming: Consider what observations and experiments can support a field's claims. (context: Cosmology gives his provocative example: "you have one sample only", the universe, without a collection of universes or experimental control over their origins. His skepticism here is his assessment of the difficulty, not a demonstration that cosmology cannot be science.)
- [02:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=164s) Hamming: Keep contradictions visible even when the accepted framework makes them easy to dismiss: "maybe what I'm being told is not the complete story". (context: He keeps apparent learning in one-cell organisms without nervous systems in mind when hearing accounts of knowledge stored in neurons. He explicitly says the example "may or may not be relevant"; noticing an anomaly creates a chance of change, not proof of a replacement theory.)
- [04:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=291s) Hamming: Do not count on a sound new idea winning acceptance within your lifetime: "we didn't convert them we outlived them". (context: He attributes the line to Planck, then recalls resistance to continental drift despite matching rocks and fossils, and the later rediscovery of Mendel's work. These are his historical accounts; he admits he cannot know how many ideas were permanently lost and gives no reliable figures for resistance to relativity or quantum mechanics.)
- [09:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=573s) Hamming: Inspect an impossibility proof's assumptions before applying it to a different method: "I'm not saying it's a good method". (context: In his patent-office anecdote, simple suction has a roughly 33-foot water limit, but a piston, standing pressure waves, and valves allegedly lift water 100 feet. The device changes the mechanism assumed by the textbook argument. This is his unverified anecdote, and he explicitly distinguishes showing possibility from recommending the engineering.)
- [11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=666s) Hamming: An elegant answer can obstruct progress when it solves the wrong problem: "the right answer to the wrong problem". (context: An energetic young colleague forced a partly familiar situation into a known pattern, solved it exactly, and left work that had to be undone. The expert missed the elements that made the actual problem different; Hamming wanted a reasonably good answer to that problem.)
- [13:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=823s) Hamming: Choose a computational representation that gives the recipient insight, not just numbers. (context: Electrical engineers could understand which frequencies passed through a calculation, while a polynomial of degree five meant little to them. He also tried real exponentials for clients who thought in those terms. He says this helped "frequently not always"; his computing colleagues' continued preference for polynomials illustrates resistance even to small methodological changes.)
- [15:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=945s) Hamming: Keeping up protects both your opportunities and how you remember your career. (context: A friend who taught him analog computing would not move to digital and left through "encouraged retirement"; Hamming left for another job with pleasant memories, while his friend felt pushed out. He also recalls a retired Navy captain who, when drunk enough, was back commanding a flotilla off South America. These are personal observations, not proof that everyone passed over failed to adapt.)
- [19:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1166s) Hamming: Take useful ideas seriously even when they come from outside the established group. (context: His memorable case is an undertaker who believed operators were distributing business unfairly and built a mechanical switch: "if I build a mechanical switch I will be bound to get fairness". He also cites carbon dating coming from physics into archaeology and Einstein working in a patent office. He qualifies the telephone story with "if I'm correct the stories I've read"; his broader claim that major advances usually come from outsiders is his judgment, not a measured rate.)
- [20:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1229s) Hamming: Choose deliberately how much time to spend on outsiders' proposals: "there is no easy answer". (context: Listening to every eccentric can consume all your time; rejecting them all may exclude the next major advance. He ties the choice to wanting a breakthrough versus continuing first-class work. Even a former collaborator with original ideas had to be told, in practice, that Hamming's interests had moved on; endless letters and calls do not create an unlimited claim on attention.)
- [23:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1419s) Hamming: The methods that earned authority can become the wrong methods after success. (context: He draws progress as a curve in many dimensions: his computing direction is almost perpendicular to his boss's earlier successful direction. "what made you successful and got you to the top is very likely to be irrelevant or wrong when you're there". The picture is an analogy for changing conditions, not a mathematical law of careers.)
- [25:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1514s) Hamming: Judge a tool by the problems people and machines can solve together: "the real problem is what can man and machine do together and not in competition". (context: Analytic mathematicians challenged his early computers with problems they themselves could solve elegantly. He wanted to solve problems they could not. He recalls repeating this "50 times or 100 times" without getting through; their opposition was often an atmosphere rather than an explicit objection, and he interpreted it partly as fear of displacement.)
- [27:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1638s) Hamming: Give the next generation room to solve its changed problems: "I try not to get in the way of the Next Generation". (context: Saving a few operations mattered when one operation took a second; the same habit could misdirect work when it took a nanosecond. Recognizing his inherited slant, he avoided computer-selection decisions unless asked for advice. He says old methods are inappropriate frequently, not always.)
- [29:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1761s) Hamming: Creating an idea does not give you permanent authority over its development. (context: Told that a friend doubted he understood error-correcting codes, he nearly replied "why that little twerp", then said "yes he's probably right". He links this to Newton opening a modern door while thinking in older ways, and to Einstein's later opposition to quantum mechanics. His claim that Einstein hindered progress is his assessment; the practical lesson is to restrain injured pride and let successors develop the idea.)
- [31:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1916s) Hamming: Revisit a failed approach when its constraints change: "I tried it once it didn't work" can preserve an obsolete conclusion. (context: Explaining his missed FFT opportunity, he says he had judged it using an earlier computing machine and repeated the verdict after acquiring a different one. He contrasts remembering the answer with rethinking why it had been true, and blames his own failure to reconsider.)
- [32:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=1947s) Hamming: An open mind still needs probability estimates: "an open mind and a hole in the head are sometimes indistinguishable". (context: He recalls incompatible possibilities of 20 feet of lunar dust swallowing a vehicle and a glassy surface making it slide. Merely being unable to disprove either was insufficient; he asks for "10% 50% 90%" rather than treating every possibility alike. These are requested judgments, not probabilities he measured or assigned to those lunar predictions.)
- [34:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2042s) Hamming: Treat his stories as fallible patterns from experience: "sometimes the experts are right and the people with new ideas are wrong". (context: He says the isolated stories illustrate situations and admits that contrary stories exist. His account of resistance to innovation is not perfect knowledge or a rule that the dissenter must be right.)
- [35:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2135s) Hamming: Evaluate failures by who repeatedly suffers them, not just the average error rate. (context: In his account of a photographic memory plate for an electronic central office, occasional flaws might misdirect one in 10,000 calls. That sounded tolerable until "it's always the same guy getting it" for a month. Error-correcting codes addressed the fixed flaws; he says this desperate application finally brought adoption after about ten years, a delay reported from his own experience.)
- [36:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2166s) Hamming: Compare the communication requirements before calling error detection a substitute for correction. (context: The objection was to detect an error and request another message, which requires two-way transmission. With error correction, "I feed the message and I feed the correction stuff with it", so the receiver already has redundancy to correct errors within the code's capability. He calls this feed-forward; it differs from waiting for feedback and a retransmission.)
- [40:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2452s) Hamming: Stop and ask whether loyalty to your local group is harming the organization: "is this the way I should be behaving"? (context: A boss tasked with learning how mechanical central offices worked reportedly spent a year and a half or two years extracting knowledge from a group that obstructed the electronic replacement. He had to ask one question to uncover the answer to another. Hamming also recalls software colleagues withholding what programs did. His explanation in terms of small-group instincts is his interpretation; the remedy is active, repeated reflection and cooperation across departments.)
- [42:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2574s) Hamming: A good idea can still be a poor use of your time when resistance makes progress unattainable: "there are times when it's not worth fighting for a good new idea". (context: He abandoned two ideas he considered good in chemistry because the group's opposition was too strong, and went to other work. He also abandoned an executive-computing effort when he could not obtain the sustained participation needed. He is advising a judgment about opportunity cost, not saying opposition proves an idea wrong.)
- [43:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWNBzAgAiMc&t=2605s) Hamming: Observe what users actually do before designing help around an imagined job. (context: For executive decision support he wanted a first-class executive three days a week for several years. He had not expected that top Bell Labs executives spent most of their time on "budget and personal squabbles", rather than scientific decisions. The request was his proposed way to learn their work, not an established minimum for every study; he judged that implementing his original assumptions would have failed.)
