# Lecture 10 - Coding Theory - The Representation of Information, Part I · notes
- [01:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=103s) Hamming: The model applies widely because it says little about the source: "Nothing is said about the source." (context: Mathematical formulas, a novel, music notation, and dance notation can all supply symbols; the theory transports those symbols without explaining their meaning.)
- [03:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=234s) Hamming: Design for errors from the start: "We're accepting at the very bottom that nothing is reliable." (context: He puts noise into the channel model rather than adding measurement error afterward. Source encoding exploits the source's structure; channel encoding helps the receiver recover what was sent despite errors. He distinguishes this noise from quantum uncertainty.)
- [06:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=388s) Hamming: Storage is communication with the future: "transmission through space and transmission through time are really the same kind of problem". (context: Writing a disk encodes the data; reading it later decodes it. The same question is whether the recovered symbols match the originals.)
- [07:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=449s) Hamming: Coding lets us escape "the necessity of doing things right because it's too expensive to do it right". (context: He recalls a commercially available differential analyzer whose cost would rise by a factor of 10 for one more binary digit of accuracy. This is his particular cost example, not a universal scaling law; coding is his alternative to making every component more precise or running a machine very slowly.)
- [12:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=767s) Hamming: A good representation makes decoding mechanical: "You only look at each bit only once." (context: Distinct codewords alone do not ensure an unambiguous concatenated message. With a prefix-free code, follow the tree until a leaf, emit its symbol, and restart: "Bing bing bing." No guessing or rereading is needed.)
- [14:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=861s) Hamming: Do not pay for extra generality when it buys nothing: "So why bother with those?" (context: Reversing his code preserves unique decoding but can force the receiver to wait for the message's end. McMillan's result allows a prefix-free code with the same codeword lengths, so instantaneous decoding need not cost average length.)
- [19:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=1195s) Hamming: The best code depends on the source: "what is going to be the best code must depend upon the frequency of the symbols being sent". (context: His long, lean tree wins when probabilities are 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/16: average length 1 7/8 bits versus 2 1/8 for the bushier tree. Equal probabilities reverse the preference: 14/5 versus 12/5. Optimize the weighted average, not the tree's appearance.)
- [21:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=1319s) Hamming: Even a long-polished proof may deserve a fresh approach: "Let's see if we can't find a simpler one." (context: Writing his coding book, he found the existing proof a mess and tried induction on a decoding tree. At 26:43 he returns to ask where unique decodability actually enters; the later counting argument covers codes without an instantaneous decoding tree.)
- [24:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=1476s) Hamming: Design how to stop, not just how to continue: "When am I done?" (context: He warns that an exit symbol is often forgotten. Reaching an ordinary leaf means emit a symbol and restart; reaching the exit means stop decoding. A compiler finishing translation and a running program finishing computation are different stops at different language levels.)
- [29:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=1764s) Hamming: Do not confuse permission to build something with proof that your construction works: "it only says that there exists a code which will do the trick". (context: A Kraft sum at most one permits a prefix-free code with those lengths; it does not make an arbitrary assignment uniquely decodable. His 9/8 example is impossible, while the later sum of one admits a code he constructs. Equality uses all the tree's capacity, but alone does not prove minimum average length for a given source.)
- [35:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2147s) Hamming: "The idea is not in the words." When an explanation fails, try different words, then a third way if needed. (context: A student explaining the lecture to a spouse or friend will use different words while trying to convey the same idea; repeating the original wording is not the only way to get it across.)
- [36:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2213s) Hamming: Understanding is inferred from "a few samples" of behavior, not directly observed. (context: He uses about three exam problems to test integration by parts, including one that requires students to recognize when to use it. On a failure he asks whether it was an algebraic slip or a failure to grasp the idea. Years of familiar behavior can still hide a different underlying idea.)
- [37:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2276s) Hamming: Producing candidates is not the same as recognizing knowledge: "we don't know how to recognize knowledge". (context: He imagines feeding a random source into a program that recognizes information; eventually even the next theory of physics would appear. The thought experiment assumes unlimited waiting, not a practical discovery method. The hard step is specifying what the recognizer should accept.)
- [41:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2468s) Hamming: Check whether a model's symbols stay fixed before applying its results to human communication: "A tall building or a tall story" use the same word differently. (context: His formal source supplies recognizable symbols, but meanings can shift even within one debate, and colleagues can hold different ideas of the same organization. His warning limits the model's application; it is not a claim that mathematics can never help study people.)
- [43:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2595s) Hamming: "Once you put something into words that becomes what you believe." Record what you saw before discussing it with others. (context: In his staged-classroom-shooting example, witnesses disagree about shots and their order. After witnessing a car accident, he typed his account before talking with his wife, "because words tend to freeze something". He connects this to a boss becoming more committed after saying no; these are his illustrations, not a claim that every verbal judgment is irreversible.)
- [45:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2750s) Hamming: A familiar representation need not be the most efficient one. (context: He describes Intel instructions of variable length: frequent instructions are short, rarer ones long. A machine can consume a bit stream, decode at a leaf, execute, and continue. His point is to consider smaller program storage rather than assume instructions must occupy equal-sized blocks; he does not claim every machine benefits.)
- [47:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNpQL8jo4BI&t=2846s) Hamming: "The greatest contributions of the greatest mathematicians are often the definition." Make an elusive idea definite enough to act on, while recognizing that "you do some damage in the definition". (context: He frames the AI question as what you can program, then asks how an organization should function before writing software to make it work that way. He closes by mentioning neural nets and fuzzy sets as alternatives he will not develop here.)
