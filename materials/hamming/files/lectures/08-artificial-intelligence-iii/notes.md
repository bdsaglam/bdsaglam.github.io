# Lecture 8 - Artificial Intelligence III · notes
- [00:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=7s) Hamming: Wanting a conclusion is not evidence for it, on either side of the machine-thinking debate: "just because you want to think machines cannot think doesn't mean that they can't". (context: He opens by making the question concrete—can you program a machine to do it?—before asking the class to defend its positions.)
- [01:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=101s) Hamming: Do not assume that a programmer must already know every move a learning program can discover. (context: Against the claim that a computer cannot have creative thought, he recalls the checker program producing moves that surprised a master; in his account, Samuel did not know how to play checkers, but his program learned. This is his example against the objection, not a proof that machines think.)
- [07:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=451s) Hamming: Apply the same test to humans and machines when you argue that dependence on outside stimulus rules out thought. (context: A student says a computer needs someone to supply its initial stimulus. Hamming counters with human sensory-deprivation experiments, describing a person floating in water without stimuli; the captions garble the reported outcome, so that outcome is not quoted here.)
- [09:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=576s) Hamming: Remember how much of your own knowledge the environment supplied before treating that dependence as a uniquely machine limitation: "the language you learned was the language you heard from the environment". (context: A student says computers cannot escape the programmer's paradigm. Hamming points to the language of the country where a child grows up; his broader claim that everything is learned from the environment is a challenge in the debate, not an established explanation of all human creativity.)
- [11:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=668s) Hamming: Distinguish making a machine obey from making it discover: AI researchers "want to get the machine to produce things we cannot". (context: A student imagines bored traffic lights wanting to become microwaves and argues that we want control rather than thinking. Hamming agrees that early machines were designed to do what we wanted, but says AI is pursuing a different goal; whether it can succeed remains the question.)
- [15:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=917s) Hamming: Look behind the moment of insight for the gradual work that prepared it: creativity is "much more slow development", often subconscious, before the moment of awareness. (context: He rejects the comic-strip light bulb as the whole story. In his account, Einstein had asked as a child what a light wave would look like if he traveled alongside it; the conflict with a stationary wave led him "Inch by Inch by Inch" toward relativity. This is Hamming's account of how almost all creative work develops, not a claim that every discovery has a fully traced history.)
- [17:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=1076s) Hamming: Ask what evidence supports claims about inner experience, including human claims: "why should I believe you"? (context: A student says a computer cannot be shown to daydream because it produces no results, yet claims to prove his own daydreaming through the emotions he feels. Hamming challenges that unequal standard; he does not demonstrate that a machine has feelings.)
- [31:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=1863s) Hamming: Turn a proposed limitation into a testable comparison: "if I gave a machine more senses than you have would it not be able to do more"? (context: After repeated objections about machines lacking the richness of human input, he proposes more sense organs plus a neural network that learns from experience, and asks whether it could then do better. This is a counterexample to consider, not a result showing that more sensors guarantee intelligence.)
- [31:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=1895s) Hamming: Do not assume machines must be unable to design machines better than themselves: "machines do design machines". (context: He cites machine-assisted chip design, including Pentium chips, saying humans gave direction while machines handled the bulk of the design and millions of connections. He extends this to machine assembly and says he is not sure machines cannot make better machines; his historical description does not establish fully autonomous design.)
- [33:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=1992s) Hamming: Predictability has been a design choice, not evidence that a machine cannot behave unpredictably: "we can easily fix up machines to be random if you want". (context: Answering a student who contrasts predictable manufactured products with unpredictable children, he proposes waiting for a cosmic ray to trigger an action. We wanted a gun director to shoot down an airplane, rather than wander off to play checkers. Randomness is the capability he defends here; he does not show that randomness alone is thought.)
- [41:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=2511s) Hamming: Be careful about dismissing performance as merely simulated intelligence when you accept human performance as real: "there are frequently times when the teacher feels a class is only simulating intelligence". (context: A student insists that a program discussing whether computers think would really be its programmer discussing it. Hamming answers with the classroom joke, challenging the distinction without resolving it.)
- [43:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaGGcMyi9BA&t=2638s) Hamming: Take part in the argument rather than staying safely silent: "those who kept quiet I don't congratulate". (context: He closes by praising the class's discussion on average and congratulating those who spoke, without announcing a winning answer to whether machines think.)
