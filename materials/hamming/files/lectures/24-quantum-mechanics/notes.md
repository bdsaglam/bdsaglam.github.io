# Lecture 24: Quantum Mechanics
- [00:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=36s) Hamming: Claims of completeness deserve a check against what remains unexplained. He challenges confident physicists with their missing "90 to 99%". (context: This is Hamming’s 1995 estimate of unaccounted-for cosmic matter, not a current measured fraction; he asks how much physics really knows despite its successes.)
- [01:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=69s) Hamming: Distinguish describing behavior from explaining its cause: "science never tells you why it tells you what". (context: Newton’s gravity tells how bodies fall without supplying a mechanism for action at a distance. Hamming contrasts Newton’s unease with our familiarity; his sweeping statement concerns this distinction, not a denial that science explains phenomena.)
- [03:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=196s) Hamming: A striking fit can be a clue to a theory, and a familiar mathematical step may be exactly what prevents it working. (context: In Hamming’s account, Planck thought "this fit is not chance". Dividing energy into finite pieces preserved the black-body distribution, but taking the pieces to zero destroyed it. He presents Planck’s willingness to retain the pieces as the decisive break with the expected limiting procedure; this is his compressed historical account.)
- [06:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=386s) Hamming: Equivalent representations need not be equally useful: "yes they're equivalent but no not quite". (context: Wave and matrix mechanics accounted for the same discrete phenomena. He compares analytic and synthetic geometry: equivalence does not mean a particular proof is equally accessible in both forms.)
- [07:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=449s) Hamming: Data need not determine a unique internal explanation: "given phenomena it is not possible to go to a unique Theory". (context: At 09:34–10:37 he recalls taking over a PhD thesis that inferred structure from random inputs and measured outputs. Different electrical circuits could have exactly the same input-output behavior under those measurements; disagreement between compatible explanations alone does not show either is wrong.)
- [08:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=481s) Hamming: Fit data using functions a collaborator could connect to a theory, rather than only functions convenient to compute. (context: Inspired by Planck, he went to lunch with scientists to learn which functions made sense in their fields instead of defaulting to polynomials. He hoped a spectacular fit would prompt discovery: "I never had that greatest success", but he says the approach did help some scientists see more.)
- [09:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=543s) Hamming: Turn the history of good work into a change in your own habits: "let me behave that way". (context: Planck’s example changed how Hamming computed for others. He did not reproduce a quantum-mechanics-sized breakthrough, but says he did useful work by deliberately creating a chance to participate in something important.)
- [12:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=764s) Hamming: Success within a tested range does not guarantee success at its boundaries. (context: Newtonian mechanics had survived centuries of tests and predicted unknown planets, yet relativity and quantum mechanics predicted better at the large/fast and small extremes he describes. His phrase "at both ends it was wrong" concerns those limits, not the loss of its usefulness in its familiar range.)
- [16:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=985s) Hamming: Consider whether a failure of intuition reflects the limits of the thinker: "there are thoughts you cannot think". (context: Dogs hear pitches we cannot, and birds see frequencies we cannot. He suggests wave-particle duality as a possible mental counterpart: our thinking developed around large everyday objects. This is his conjecture about cognitive limits, not a proof that quantum mechanics cannot be understood.)
- [18:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1108s) Hamming: Different descriptions can serve different questions without either being useless. (context: A tabletop is solid for an elasticity problem and mostly empty space in his atomic description. He uses it to explain complementary descriptions, while calling the dominant interpretation a "dominant religion" and himself "a heretic" at 18:59–19:33. The religious analogy and his account of physicists’ allegiances are his polemic, not a verified history or a refutation of the theory.)
- [20:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1205s) Hamming: Ask what a probability means in the problem at hand; do not silently switch between a unique event and repeated trials. (context: For the risk that a bomb might ignite the atmosphere, "you either do it or you don't"—there is no practical series of repeated catastrophes. He then points to interference with roughly one photon in the apparatus at a time. His conclusion about how quantum probability ought to be interpreted is his position, not an experimental resolution of the meaning of probability.)
- [22:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1331s) Hamming: Build and test a predictive structure even before you have a satisfying intuitive account: early researchers were "feeling their way forward". (context: He describes quantum theory growing by explaining a few things and then more. At 25:45–26:15 he imagines a model of the mind whose meaning its maker cannot explain: "it successfully predicts". He explicitly calls that situation unsatisfactory; predictive usefulness and intuitive understanding are distinct achievements.)
- [23:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1423s) Hamming: Check whether your usual way of simplifying a system actually applies. (context: Circuit analysis becomes manageable with loosely connected black boxes; statistical mechanics exploits repetition. He argues that the brain offers neither easy separation nor sufficient regularity for those straightforward approaches. This is his warning about the limits of the proposed models, not a demonstration that either field can contribute nothing to neuroscience.)
- [24:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1484s) Hamming: A failure caused by disabling a part does not establish that the function originates there. (context: If someone in Kansas cuts the telephone line while you are talking to your mother in New York, "it may be only the transmission line". He uses the analogy to question simple localization of thought, then invokes learning in one-celled organisms as a reason to question identifying all learning with a nervous system; the biological example is his claim here.)
- [27:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1637s) Hamming: Read an impossibility proof together with its assumptions. (context: In his account of successive arguments over hidden variables, he warns that "any proof of that kind of thing must rest on a large number of other assumptions". His sweeping summary of proofs and disproofs is not an unrestricted verdict about every possible hidden-variable theory; the lesson is to examine what a theorem actually rules out.)
- [32:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=1952s) Hamming: Do not make a lasting philosophical commitment depend heavily on a current scientific interpretation: "if we change our ideas of science God changes" is the consequence he finds implausible. (context: After discussing randomness, free will, punishment, and mercy, he doubts that contemporary quantum mechanics can settle these questions. His examples include an infinitely merciful Buddha forgiving even an evil life and religious intermediaries seeming to have "a contract with God"; these are his characterizations, not checked accounts of those religions. At 33:02–34:07 he leaves the mind–matter interaction problem unresolved.)
- [35:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2142s) Hamming: Separate a startling effect from what it lets you communicate, and ask the question while you have the chance. (context: Discussing Aspect’s entanglement experiments, he acknowledges that the correlations do not provide useful faster-than-light signaling. His analogy is a searchlight spot sweeping faster than light without sending a message between successive illuminated places. When Aspect declined a relativity question, Hamming stayed silent: "I've kicked myself ever since". His language of instantaneous effects is his interpretation, not evidence of a usable superluminal signal.)
- [38:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2301s) Hamming: Keep a theory’s predictive success separate from claims about what measurement ultimately means. (context: Hamming challenges a consciousness-based collapse account by asking what state the universe had before consciousness arose, then tells the cat-in-a-box story: "the cat is neither dead nor alive until you look". He calls quantum mechanics successful but philosophically headache-producing. His designation of consciousness as "official doctrine" is his characterization of an interpretation, not a universal premise of quantum mechanics.)
- [39:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2396s) Hamming: A familiar word can conceal a problem you cannot yet specify: "I think I know what understanding is" until asked to explain it. (context: He invokes Augustine’s puzzle about time, then says he has no idea how to write a program that does understanding, despite being confident that he understands some things. The programming challenge exposes the gap between using the word and giving an explicit account of it; his inability is not an impossibility theorem.)
- [41:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2488s) Hamming: Trust intuition according to how well its training circumstances match the present problem. (context: When an expert disagrees with a simulation, his rule is to favor experience in slowly changing technology and the simulation in rapidly changing technology: "intuition is based on the past". At 42:30–43:01 he recalls successful mathematicians resisting computers. This is his decision heuristic, not a reason to trust an unvalidated simulation automatically.)
- [44:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2644s) Hamming: When tools change what students can do, reconsider what class should require—and distinguish feasibility from educational value. (context: He forecasts that by 2020 a student’s laptop could hold the textbooks, techniques, differentiation, and integration tools needed for study. Yet "whether we should or not it's another problem", and he admits "I waffle". He criticizes slow adoption while acknowledging that institutions cannot change overnight; the date is his 1995 forecast.)
- [45:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2735s) Hamming: New experimental control can turn a theoretical assertion into something testable; also inspect what your mathematical model already assumes. (context: He imagines tagging a single atom and observing which slit it traverses, then connects the position–momentum uncertainty relation to the Fourier-transform uncertainty theorem. These are his proposed experimental prospect and modeling argument, not a reported violation of the uncertainty principle. His claim that every linear theory must have such a principle should be read in the conjugate-variable setting he describes.)
- [47:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6oxz3ykLWLI&t=2861s) Hamming: Do not let what is easiest to teach displace what matters: "we tend to tell you what we know". (context: He says people and morale are central to his military students’ work and would prefer a high-morale team to better weapons with low morale—his emphatic judgment. Hardware dominates teaching partly because teachers know how to explain it. He deliberately includes unresolved questions instead: at 48:11, "my job is to make you think".)
