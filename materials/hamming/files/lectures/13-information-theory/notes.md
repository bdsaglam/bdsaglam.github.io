# Lecture 13 - Information Theory · notes
- [01:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=66s) Hamming: Choose a measure for the job, and notice what its assumptions leave out. Shannon identifies information with surprise: "if a certain event happens then you've learned nothing". (context: Hamming says communication theory would be a more accurate name. Adding information for independent die and coin outcomes, and requiring continuity, leads to the logarithmic measure; it does not establish a measure of human meaning.)
- [05:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=351s) Hamming: "The two things have the same algebraic form does not mean that the variables have the same meaning." (context: Entropy in communication and statistical mechanics has the same formula. Hamming repeats an explicitly unverified story that von Neumann suggested the name because "nobody understands entropy anyhow" and "you'll always win in any argument"; a shared formula or impressive name does not establish a shared interpretation.)
- [10:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=634s) Hamming: Use a theoretical bound to know what improvement is possible: "entropy gives a lower bound you can't do better than that". (context: From Gibbs' and Kraft's inequalities he bounds the average length of a uniquely decodable, symbol-by-symbol code. A loaded die is more predictable than a balanced die, so its outcomes have less entropy and can require fewer bits on average.)
- [18:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=1083s) Hamming: Distinguish an error count from an error percentage: "when it's very small I expect big variations when is large I expect small variation". (context: Six heads out of ten would not surprise him; 600,000 out of a million would. Absolute fluctuations grow roughly as the square root of the number of trials, while their fraction shrinks. This lets a slightly enlarged decoding radius capture almost all outcomes of the independent-noise model.)
- [25:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=1501s) Hamming: Separate a proof of possibility from an engineering design: "we're talking Theory can it be done not is it practical". (context: Shannon makes codewords by tossing coins and averages over all possible codebooks. Hamming says a codebook near capacity with small error would "fill the solar system" if printed on paper; this is his illustration of its scale, not a quantified size estimate.)
- [28:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=1720s) Hamming: When a derivation stays opaque, try deriving the result yourself: "I do what I normally do and I advise you to do it". (context: Sources kept referring him to a "well-known sum", and the derivation he finally found was too complicated to read. He estimated the largest binomial coefficient with Stirling's formula, bounded the remaining terms with a geometric series, then recognized entropy by taking logarithms.)
- [31:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=1908s) Hamming: An average can prove that something exists without telling you how to find it: "if the average is arbitrarily good there must be at least one good one". (context: Averaging error over all random codebooks proves there is a code arbitrarily close to channel capacity with arbitrarily small error, for sufficiently long blocks. The final existence step supplies no practical construction or search procedure.)
- [32:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=1971s) Hamming: Test what "sufficiently large" costs in use: "if he really wants to send out SOS you got to wait". (context: Hamming recalls trying roughly a dozen or more small random codes with desk calculators, then removing badly placed codewords; none came near the desired performance. His deliberately extreme SOS example waits for "another billion letters" before encoding. The lesson is to count collection delay as well as transmission efficiency, not to treat that number as a universal minimum.)
- [33:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2033s) Hamming: Capacity you rarely use is wasted: "if I build a system which will correct 10 errors and almost all the time is only correcting five I'm wasting capacity". (context: Hamming uses this comparison to motivate long blocks that correct many errors in weak deep-space transmissions. He describes probes with only a few watts and codes correcting around 100 or more errors per block; these are his lecture illustrations, not independently checked spacecraft specifications.)
- [36:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2191s) Hamming: Look for structure that replaces an impossible lookup table: "you don't construct the code book you calculate what the correct answer was". (context: Shannon's random codebook requires an enormous explicit listing. Hamming codes and the carefully designed deep-space codes instead compute encoding and decoding through regular rules; existence theory points toward the goal, while structure makes an implementation possible.)
- [37:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2252s) Hamming: Design for the source of interference: "when you have an active enemy you do something else". (context: Deep-space noise calls for one approach; a local jammer deliberately knocks out bits and motivates coding plus frequency hopping known to the receiver. Hamming attributes to Einstein the remark that nature is not malicious but can be difficult; the attribution is not verified here.)
- [38:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2316s) Hamming: Human information depends on the receiver's knowledge: "whether you're learning something or not depends upon your state of knowledge". (context: A number stream surprises you until you know its generator. A teacher's few words can suddenly make a confused subject clear to some students but not others, and repeating the explanation in different words may help. Surprise alone does not capture understanding or insight.)
- [40:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2413s) Hamming: Inspect a definition when you make it and again after deriving its consequences: "how much follows for the arbitrary definition"? (context: Hamming describes Binet retaining correlated questions, then a grading calibration that makes IQ scores normally distributed: "hell you made it normally distributed". This is his critique of taking a property built into scoring as a discovery about intelligence, not a verified history of IQ testing or a claim that scores measure nothing.)
- [43:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2605s) Hamming: Try a theory in a new field and test whether its assumptions fit: "the only way to find out is try". (context: Hamming reports "many interesting things and many interesting failures" from applying information theory beyond communication machines. He speculates about whether genetic mixing involves something beyond the mechanical. That is his speculation; failure of an application alone does not establish nonmechanical causes.)
- [44:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2665s) Hamming: Question independence before paying to transmit everything. (context: Television compression saves bandwidth because neighboring picture elements are correlated: "originally you thought every spot was independent but they weren't". Looking at joint structure changes how much must be sent, even though the displayed picture still has the same number of spots.)
- [44:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YF2wOE66gfw&t=2697s) Hamming: Do not mistake the limits of your instrument for limits of the world: "the instrument limits what you can see". (context: In Eddington's story, fishermen catch no fish below their net's mesh size and conclude that the sea contains no smaller fish. Hamming extends the warning to definitions and the mind itself: its wiring may limit what we can think or notice, just as a net filters what we catch.)
