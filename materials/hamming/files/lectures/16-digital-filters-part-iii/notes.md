# Lecture 16 - Digital Filters, Part III · notes
- [00:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=35s) Hamming: Explain to communicate, rather than to display everything you know: "the speaker is showing off how much he knows and is not really trying to communicate with you". (context: Kaiser described many filter-design methods over lunch before putting one dependable method on a lunch mat. Hamming asked "why'd you tell me the other ones" and discarded the rest. His opening claim that it always works is qualified by the ripple exceptions at 05:15.)
- [04:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=284s) Hamming: Use the computer as an experimental tool in theoretical work: "a combination of theory and using the computer to get a formula". (context: Kaiser designed and measured many filters, then fitted practical formulas. He tried exponent 0.5, found it did not work, tried 0.4, and stopped when it fitted well; the transcript garbles the second decimal, but the surrounding question and chapter confirm 0.4.)
- [05:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=315s) Hamming: A useful empirical design rule still needs its output checked; repair a known failure instead of treating it as a guarantee. (context: Kaiser fitted a single filter edge, but a band-pass filter has four edges including negative frequencies. Their ripples can reinforce and exceed the requested tolerance. Hamming says to reduce the tolerance and repeat, usually once rather than ten times.)
- [12:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=730s) Hamming: Remember why something is impossible, so you can reconsider when its assumptions change: "I merely remembered it couldn't be done". (context: Tukey suggested the FFT ideas when Hamming had a card-programmed calculator; repeatedly removing and sorting cards made the butterfly operations impractical. With an internally programmed 650 he remembered only that it was a bad idea and missed the opportunity. He extends the warning to senior officers whose judgments may assume obsolete weapons.)
- [14:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=887s) Hamming: Check numerical accuracy when a faster algorithm appears; speed and accuracy need not trade off. (context: For 10,000 data points, Hamming contrasts 100 million operations at quadratic scale with roughly N log N for the FFT. He expected a clever shortcut to worsen roundoff, investigated, and found the FFT gave more accurate coefficients with less computation. This is his account, not a universal guarantee about every FFT implementation.)
- [16:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1013s) Hamming: Know the representation your computation imposes: with an FFT, "you are asserting this function in this interval is periodic". (context: The sampling interval times the sample count sets the assumed period, often unrelated to the source. A sinusoid that does not fit that period spreads into other frequency bins, strongest nearby. The FFT computes the finite transform accurately; interpreting its bins as untouched spectral lines of the original signal is the mistake.)
- [18:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1137s) Hamming: Ask what information a convenient representation discards: "you're losing all the phase that's almost half the information". (context: A power spectrum combines the sine and cosine coefficients into a magnitude that does not depend on the time origin, but loses their relative phase. His roughly-half count compares independent coefficients, not a universal information-theoretic percentage; phase-sensitive signaling is a case where that discarded part matters.)
- [21:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1292s) Hamming: Choose what to preserve by the task: "you've got to ask which of the two am I in". (context: Hamming contrasts a pilot understanding speech through cockpit noise with a musician making fine distinctions. While building Heathkit hi-fi equipment to learn electronics, he encountered the limits of carrying voice-telephony assumptions about phase into music reproduction. His account allows some phase sensitivity; it does not say the ear never hears phase.)
- [22:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1357s) Hamming: Experiments can reveal a useful model without uniquely revealing the mechanism: "there are different equivalent structures which will sound exactly the same". (context: In a thesis he took over after a student-professor quarrel, input-output tests could constrain a black box but not determine its internal topology uniquely. Different circuits can have the same response; injecting roughly white noise into a chemical plant can still help build a useful process model.)
- [24:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1485s) Hamming: After detrending, name the quantity you actually analyzed: "this is a spectrum of the difference between the straight line and the data". (context: For Tukey's radar data, Hamming removed lines and sometimes parabolas. Spectra involve squares, so spectra do not simply add: a signal and its negative have the same power spectrum but sum to zero. When Tukey said "shut up Hammy", Hamming took it to mean he had no remedy either; that interpretation is Hamming's, not an admission by Tukey.)
- [26:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1612s) Hamming: Do not mistake processing artifacts for properties of the source: "they interpreted the result of processing to be the original source". (context: Removing the mean suppresses the zero-frequency component; subsequent windowing redistributes nearby spectral energy. Hamming recalls hearing that most energy lay near the third frequency and realizing their processing had put it there. His move from urgent industrial problems to university reflection gave him time to question this familiar pattern.)
- [27:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1643s) Hamming: Even turning an instrument on and off is a processing choice; a finite observation smears spectral lines. (context: The on/off interval is a rectangular or boxcar window. Hamming stresses that every pure spectral line is broadened, with a longer record making the smearing narrower. Sampling then aliases higher frequencies into lower ones, so the observed spectrum reflects both the source and the measurement process; his low-frequency signal plus flat noise picture is described as typical, not inevitable.)
- [29:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1772s) Hamming: Sampling faster than the minimum can buy noise reduction, but know the separation limit: "there is no linear method which will separate the noise and the signal in the same band". (context: Oversampling puts the folding frequency farther above the desired low-frequency signal, leaving more out-of-band noise to remove with a low-pass filter. His warning concerns separating overlapping signal and noise by this frequency-filtering approach, not a ban on every use of additional statistical or structural information.)
- [31:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=1865s) Hamming: Check the assumptions behind an inference, rather than continuing because "we don't know what else to do". (context: Hamming describes millions spent predicting carrier-deck motion with a linear wave model, yielding essentially nothing in his account. He also rejects treating a flat stock-price spectrum as proof that all prediction is impossible: it discards phase and does not settle nonlinear prediction. His broad statement that Fourier analysis assumes a linear mechanism applies to that modeling inference; Fourier transforms themselves can analyze signals from nonlinear systems.)
- [33:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2022s) Hamming: A prediction problem can be an information-access problem, not just an algorithm problem: "it's not a game for amateurs". (context: As a company director, Hamming obeyed a lawyer's warning not to trade on or disclose a planned stock split. Suspecting a leak anyway, he was told by the chairman that prospectus printers passed copies to brokers. This is his anecdote, not independently verified evidence about all markets; his claim that splits then doubled investors' money is likewise a historical claim, not a promised return.)
- [36:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2211s) Hamming: When errors grow, distinguish the sensitivity of the problem from a defect in the method: "the problem was unstable not the method". (context: Computing lunar landings initially alarmed him because errors could grow quadratically. With no atmospheric drag to provide the corrective effect he describes for Earth, a persistent acceleration error accumulates into quadratic position error. He corrects himself mid-explanation at 36:20; this is not a claim that a fixed initial velocity error alone gives quadratic growth.)
- [37:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2273s) Hamming: Reconsider definitions when moving a field into a new setting: "when a field of knowledge is extended to a new area the old definitions are often inappropriate". (context: He realized his predictor-corrector work already involved recursive digital filters. Analog engineers wanted bounded inputs to give bounded outputs to avoid melting hardware, but integrating even a constant produces unbounded output. For his integration work he therefore allowed polynomial growth while rejecting exponential growth; he presents this as his different criterion, not the standard bounded-input/bounded-output definition.)
- [40:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2401s) Hamming: Recheck physical limitations when the representation changes: "the numbers are still exactly the same". (context: Hamming says his analog-computer amplifiers drifted within ten minutes, forcing solutions into two or three minutes. A digital machine could hold the same numbers for two days, and its stored coefficients did not change with temperature like analog components. This concerns retained digital values, not freedom from roundoff or every numerical error.)
- [42:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2555s) Hamming: "The received knowledge developed in the past for past purposes fitting past situations was probably correct; it may not be correct for today and it is quite probably not correct for tomorrow". (context: Bell Labs' authority helped turn a voice-telephony practice of discarding phase into a wider habit. Hamming objects that other applications were not voice, and some systems now worked solely with phase. The lesson is to recheck the purpose behind an inherited practice, including one from a respected institution.)
- [43:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2615s) Hamming: Survival alone is weak evidence that we understand how to manage a new technology: "we have no evidence". (context: Leaving Los Alamos, Hamming expected atomic war within twenty years; almost fifty years without it surprised him. He explicitly says this does not establish whether the military had understood or controlled nuclear weapons. His forecast and assessment are his own, not a measured probability or an externally verified historical conclusion.)
- [45:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWhECe9MKWc&t=2709s) Hamming: Develop judgment about real novelty without chasing every novelty claim: distinguish something fundamental from "the same old stuff with a new coat of varnish". (context: He rejects both calling nuclear weapons merely bigger bombs and treating them as a solution to everything; guided missiles likewise brought new possibilities and losses. Familiar analogies help within reason, but dismissing everything new prevents contribution, while believing every claim leaves you jumping from one thing to another.)
