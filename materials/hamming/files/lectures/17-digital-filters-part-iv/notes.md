# Lecture 17 - Digital Filters, Part IV · notes
- [02:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=141s) Hamming: Check whether a traditional restriction belongs to your problem: on recorded data, "the future is right down there on the tape". (context: An airplane predictor has only past and present measurements. Offline experiments have later samples, and an image has neighbors on all sides; do not throw away that information merely because the standard filter is one-sided.)
- [05:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=330s) Hamming: Feedback can fail through delay or overcorrection: "either you can call it too strong or too late". (context: Large shower pipes delayed the hot water; he kept turning it up, climbed in, then had to hop out as the earlier adjustments arrived. A learner driver yanking the wheel from the curb toward another car makes the same mistake. In a recursive filter, errors can circulate and grow.)
- [10:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=625s) Hamming: When taught that something always holds, ask "is it true" and seek a counterexample. Experts "took what they were told in class" and "they never really questioned". (context: He reports finding a recursive filter whose impulse response ends after finitely many steps, contrary to treating every recursive filter as necessarily infinite-response. He explicitly says it need not be a filter one would want to build; the counterexample tests the universal claim.)
- [14:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=846s) Hamming: "Find the people who are doing important things and help them get important results." Judge both the person and the problem before committing your effort. (context: A colleague casually mentioned a physicist needing derivatives of noisy nuclear counts at 256 energy values. Hamming insisted on visiting the lab: careful shielding, a week-long data run, and their discussion convinced him the experimenter was capable and the problem mattered to physics and Bell Labs. His dismissal of most other work as unimportant is his judgment.)
- [15:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=942s) Hamming: Get the experimenter's model, "calculate some pseudo data based on your model", and compare its analysis with real data. (context: Both spectra put the signal at the low-frequency end and flat noise above it: "a miracle theory and practice agree". This agreement supported choosing a low-pass differentiator; differentiation amplifies high frequencies, so simply differentiating the noisy counts would worsen the problem.)
- [17:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1036s) Hamming: Give collaborators a usable method they can run themselves. (context: Kaiser supplied a program that accepted the desired cutoff, designed and windowed the differentiating filter, and processed the data. At 39:40–40:11 Hamming credits this as the right deliverable because the appropriate cutoff could differ between runs; that does not justify searching within a run without accounting for the tuning.)
- [17:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1066s) Hamming: "If I let you play and adjust everything you'll get any answer you want." Account for the freedom used in tuning when judging an answer's accuracy. (context: The physicist moved the cutoff on different parts of the same data to obtain better-looking results. Hamming called this "statistical sin"; the warning concerns unaccounted data-dependent adjustment, not a ban on choosing a cutoff.)
- [18:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1097s) Hamming: Match the data representation to the noise before combining measurements. (context: Hamming urged using square roots of the nuclear counts to equalize their variances. Even after the method worked, he had to press "four or five or six times" to get this refinement accepted. His equal-variance wording is an approximation for counting noise, not an exact property at every count level or a promise to eliminate noise.)
- [20:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1220s) Hamming: Respect an experimenter's practical judgment: he "very well may know better than you do what is the right stuff to measure". (context: Another physicist, whom Hamming says he disliked, took measurements at progressively wider time intervals. Hamming initially found that strange, but the eventual analysis showed they were exactly the measurements needed; theoretical fluency and personal liking were poor substitutes for judging the work.)
- [21:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1283s) Hamming: Connecting the right specialists and keeping them "statistically honest" can be a substantial contribution. (context: He identified the counts problem, brought Kaiser and the physicist together, and watched the assumptions rather than doing all the work. Increasing specialization leaves a need for someone to "see the bigger picture". He learned that role from Tukey; wartime demands pushed his generation out of their "comfortable ruts" and forced them to learn new subjects.)
- [24:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1440s) Hamming: To spread a useful capability, show people how their own area could benefit: "you aren't getting your money's worth". (context: He told departmental leaders that computing was being charged across Bell Labs, later to their vice-presidential areas, while they were receiving little benefit. Within weeks problems arrived from those areas. He deliberately used this incentive to spread computing throughout the laboratory.)
- [25:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1531s) Hamming: Use simple filters to answer practical questions, including questions outside your specialty. (context: For Navy leaders he proposes smoothing noisy records of ship tonnage versus firepower to see larger trends. Over a career, "10 or 20 different filters" might answer 10 or 20 questions. His point is that many useful filters could be brief, small jobs rather than the elaborate designs needed for gun directors; this is advice, not a measured frequency of use.)
- [27:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1661s) Hamming: Choose a filter that preserves the change you need to see: a running median "will follow a sudden change in state". (context: In an odd-sized window across two flat levels, the median switches when the new level gains the majority. Unlike smoothing that spreads a jump or produces ringing, this can retain the transition. He connects it to radar switching from searching for a target to tracking it; the example is not a guarantee for every noisy discontinuity.)
- [29:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1755s) Hamming: Check the noise model as well as the signal model. (context: He distinguishes additive noise, which the linear filtering discussion assumes, from noise whose size grows with the measured value, as in floating-point quantities. A method suited to fixed-size additive errors needs reconsideration when the error scales with the data; this passage has garbled captions, so the distinction is paraphrased.)
- [31:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1879s) Hamming: Do not dismiss an important problem as "none of my business". Leadership needs someone "conscious of the needs of the whole not of the parts". (context: The hallway remark about physics became his responsibility because he worked for Bell Labs, not just its math department. His contrasting sales-led and manufacturing-led companies illustrate how optimizing one department can leave the whole organization worse off; these are his illustrative scenarios.)
- [32:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=1976s) Hamming: Protect the trust that lets people bring you their ideas: "one of the things you certainly want is people will trust you". (context: He left experimenters free to publish alone or invite him as co-author, and avoided writing up their work himself, even when he thought it chiefly mathematical. Otherwise, he feared, "I would have acquired the reputation of stealing other people's ideas". This was his collaboration practice, not a universal authorship rule.)
- [34:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2099s) Hamming: Demonstrate reliability before expecting greater responsibility: "you won't be an airline pilot until you are reliable beforehand". (context: In hiring, he valued dependability over brilliant grades. A young person skipped work to stay home or fish, yet promised to become reliable as a pilot. Hamming wanted people who could help during a crunch; he also says the organization should reciprocate with time off later.)
- [37:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2224s) Hamming: Breadth takes deliberate practice: "luck favors the prepared mind", and preparation means knowledge ready to be used differently. (context: He sought problems in other departments and learned unfamiliar jargon. He acknowledges luck, but also says he behaved in ways that made opportunities possible; his wider view did not arise just by staying in his original specialty.)
- [41:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2472s) Hamming: Have the courage to say "I don't understand" until you know what you are doing. (context: For ten years he served as Tukey's "chief stooge in computing". Tukey was "in the habit of snowing people"; by being "observant and tough", Hamming usually obtained an explanation at his level and "a marvelous education". He admits that a couple of times Tukey simply required the computation anyway.)
- [42:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2566s) Hamming: "Start trying to fight down compartmentalization": whenever you learn something, ask where else it applies. (context: Kaiser needed energy and counts relabeled as time and voltage before recognizing the filtering problem. Louisville students recognized the logarithm integral in calculus but not in thermodynamics across the hall. Tukey connected radar polarization to Hamming's existing knowledge of light polarization. These examples develop the same transfer problem across the lecture.)
- [43:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2598s) Hamming: Information meant to improve a system can destabilize it when people react to it. (context: Bell Labs posted the previous week's cafeteria queue lengths so people could avoid busy periods. Everyone shifted toward another time: "you never saw such an unstable method in your life". After three or four weeks the attempt was abandoned. He treats it as feedback, applying the same principle beyond electronic circuits.)
- [45:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2724s) Hamming: Keep thinking about the problem beyond scheduled work, then look beyond the setting that produced the idea. (context: Hamming recounts H. S. Black sketching the feedback idea on a newspaper during a ferry crossing: "he was thinking all the time". He says Black first saw it in electronic amplifiers; its wider applications had to be recognized later. This is Hamming's historical account, not an independently checked history.)
- [46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oA5x6Ur02lI&t=2786s) Hamming: Recognizing a shared principle does not mean every application is solved: "all the questions are not answered". (context: He spent many hours trying to predict stability with multiple feedback loops in his differential-equation work and says he could not do it. This qualifies the reach of the feedback tools discussed here; it is a statement about his difficulty, not a claim that no such analysis is possible.)
