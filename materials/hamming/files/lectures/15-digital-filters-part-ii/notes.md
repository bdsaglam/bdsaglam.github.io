# Lecture 15 - Digital Filters, Part II · notes
- [00:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=36s) Hamming: Do not dismiss a change in scale as merely faster old machinery. Hamming asserts that a tenfold change brings "fundamentally new effects". (context: He contrasts walking at about 4 mph, driving at 40, and flying at 400–500: each changes where a vacation is possible. He puts computing's gain at roughly a billionfold and says contributors treated it as something new. The broad tenfold rule is his heuristic, not a universal law.)
- [04:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=261s) Hamming: Understand an abstract mechanism by building the smallest case and checking actual numbers: "so you will see how it happens". (context: He designs a filter that sums three consecutive inputs and divides by two. It preserves a cosine at 1/6 cycle per sample, kills one at 1/3, and extracts the first from their sum. Frequency separation becomes something the reader can verify by arithmetic, not just accept from a formula.)
- [11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=707s) Hamming: Check what a nominal specification hides: "60 Cycles isn't 60 Cycles". (context: A nearby wax-paper plant started its motors, loading their shared generator and briefly slowing it before control caught up. Their analog-computing runs sometimes had to be abandoned and restarted. He says the frequency might wander from 55 to 65; that is his warning in this anecdote, not a verified general range for power grids. An accurate end-of-day clock does not establish constant frequency throughout the day.)
- [15:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=956s) Hamming: Investigate a careful experimenter's anomaly before blaming the apparatus. (context: In his account, Michelson built a machine using 75 Fourier coefficients and found an overshoot when reconstructing a curve with a jump. After equipment adjustments failed, mathematicians blamed "bad experimentation"; Gibbs of Yale took the puzzle seriously. Hamming corrects his initial Harvard slip to Yale.)
- [16:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=989s) Hamming: More terms need not remove the error you care about: "that Peak does not go to zero it gets narrower". (context: For the ordinary truncated Fourier reconstruction at a jump, he says the overshoot stays around 9% of the jump even as its neighborhood shrinks. This concerns Gibbs overshoot in that reconstruction; his later smoothing changes the approximation and reduces it.)
- [18:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=1083s) Hamming: A published discovery may fail to spread until others are ready: "people were ready to hear it". (context: Hamming tells of a continuity claim in Cauchy's book contradicted by its own Fourier example, of uniform convergence being identified and then lost from common use, and of Gibbs rediscovering the issue. This is Hamming's historical account, not an independently checked chronology. Uniform convergence would preserve continuity, but its failure alone does not prove the particular overshoot or its 9% height.)
- [27:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=1650s) Hamming: An observation changes what you can see: "anytime you start an instrument and stop it again you have multiplied the signal". (context: Turning a telescope on and off applies a finite window. In the spectrum this convolves each sharp line with a spread-out pattern, like optical diffraction. A measured spectral smudge can therefore come from the observation window rather than from a spread of frequencies in the star itself.)
- [29:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=1778s) Hamming: Choose the experiment's duration to resolve the distinction you need: "if two lines are close" you may "only get to see one". (context: A longer observation narrows the smearing from the finite window. Two close spectral peaks need sufficient separation or a sufficiently long run to appear separately; he stresses that the required resolution is calculable.)
- [30:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=1841s) Hamming: Try a small change and examine both its gain and its cost: "that small modification produced a big damping but a slight broadening". (context: He changes just the two endpoint weights of a rectangular window from one to one-half, then finds the construction already in Zygmund's book as a modified series. The damping improves while the response broadens; earlier, Lanczos' averaging likewise reduces ripples while widening a sharp transition. Rediscovering the idea still gives him a way to reason about better windows.)
- [32:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=1935s) Hamming: Check smoothness across the boundary, not only inside the interval: "picture the 4 Series on a cylinder". (context: The ends meet in a periodic Fourier representation. He connects smoother matching to faster coefficient decay, then replaces a window with corners by a raised cosine. A smooth-looking interior does not repair a mismatch where the ends join.)
- [35:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2138s) Hamming: Use physical meaning to challenge a numerical result: "that radar dish is not pulling energy out of the atmosphere". (context: His calculation of a noisy spectrum gave negative values. Rather than accept the output, he noticed that weighted smoothing of consecutive values made all three runs positive. This is what happened in his data, not a proof that such smoothing fixes every negative spectral estimate.)
- [36:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2200s) Hamming: Let being beaten push you into thinking rather than blaming the other person: "when you come in the second you don't blame the other guy". (context: Inspired by a story in which someone used a week's prepared calculations to surprise von Neumann, Hamming staged three smoothed spectra for the usually faster Tukey. He says Tukey knew he had been set up, leaned back, and in about five minutes created the theory of windows. This is Hamming's humorous recollection of their rivalry, not a literal claim that the entire field originated in those minutes.)
- [38:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2294s) Hamming: Design for the interference that can actually overwhelm your measurement: "how can I keep the max down"? (context: A strong line elsewhere in the spectrum could leak through a window's sidelobes and dominate the frequency they wanted to inspect. Tukey asked for the smallest possible maximum sidelobe, leading to the window Hamming calls a raised cosine on a platform. Its criterion came from this strong-line problem; a named window is not automatically best for every task.)
- [39:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2357s) Hamming: Useful contributions gain recognition through the people you help: "the people with whom you work do it". (context: Tukey insisted on naming the window after Hamming for his many small contributions. Hamming had joked that real fame meant a lowercase name, like watt or volt; Tukey's book printed a lowercase h. The joke makes his point about colleagues giving credit concrete.)
- [40:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2424s) Hamming: Seek working company from which you learn and to which you can contribute: "they were doing important things I helped them a lot". (context: He left the math lunch table, where games, boomerangs, and kites were fascinating but taught him little for his work, and joined the physicists. On paper placemats he sketched direction fields: if all solutions ran to infinity while the proposed physics could not, the equation was missing something.)
- [43:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WO4XLz8wFqM&t=2583s) Hamming: Make fundamentals your own by repeatedly returning to why they work: "make it your own by re digesting the thing until it is yours". (context: He contrasts remembered explanations with understanding usable in a new situation. He describes going back to calculus' foundations whenever he did an integral, and portrays Feynman as thinking physics through for himself—even joking that he would not simply believe Jesus Christ. The point is active understanding after teaching, not rejecting everything learned from others.)
